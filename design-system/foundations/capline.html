The Cap Line

Every object in this system stands on a line. Type does too — and until this chapter the system measured to the bottom of the box the browser wrapped around it instead, which is a different place by a slice of the font that no token names and no gate could see.

Where this comes from

The Found State settled the same argument one register over and wrote the sentence this chapter is the rest of: an isometric object has no bounding box either. It has a lattice edge under it. That is why a match is drawn as a ground line and not as a box, and it is exactly as true of a word that is not a match. A line of type has two real edges — the cap line a capital starts at and the baseline it stands on — and one edge that is an artefact of layout: the bottom of the line box, which sits below the baseline by however much room the font wants for its descenders.

Nothing in assets/source/manual/ draws a line box, because no manual does; it is not a thing a designer sets, it is a thing a browser makes. So this chapter is derived from the language the way The Found State and Plot are — out of parts the manual does establish. The part it is built out of is the one the space scale rests on: a number in this system is the number on the screen. Between a line of type and anything under it, it was not.

The capability arrived while this system was being built, and it is the third one in a row that did.

text-box-trim shipped in Safari 18.2, then Edge 132 and Chrome 133, and last in Firefox 154 — which is what made it worth adopting rather than noting. The Found State's three registers became Baseline inside the same window. A system that states its distances in tokens now has a way to make the type honour them, and this chapter is that.

The slot

A block containing one line of type is as tall as its line-height, and the glyphs are placed inside that height by the font's own ascent and descent. What is left over is leading, and it is split above and below. The slice above the cap line is air between this line and whatever precedes it. The slice below the baseline is the descender slot — the room g and p need — and it is present whether or not the line contains a descender.

So a padding-bottom under a line of type does not start at the baseline. It starts under the slot, and the drawn distance is the token plus the slot.

The two ink lines are not positioned, they are measured — by the property this chapter is about, out of the same font at the same size in the same box. A block trimmed trim-end and anchored to the line box's top ends on the baseline, so its lower border is one of them; a block trimmed trim-both and anchored to that lower edge starts on the cap line, so its upper border is the other. Both at text-box-edge: cap alphabetic. There is no length anywhere in the figure. A browser that cannot trim draws both blocks at the line box's own height, and the figure collapses to one pair of lines — which is the correct thing for it to say, because on that browser there is no slot to take off.

The two slots are not halves of the leading, and the split is not computable.

Read the last two rows together. Between --leading-normal and --leading-relaxed at 11 px the line box grows by 1.64 px and all of it lands under the baseline: the over slot is 3.19 px in both. The cause is that the engine places the baseline on a whole pixel — measured at 9, 10, 11, 11, 15, 17, 27 and 34 px from the box top across the rows above, every one of them an integer — and the rounding is absorbed by whichever slot is left over.

So the descender slot is not (line-height − content) / 2 + descent. It is what remains after a rounded baseline, at this size, in this font, in this engine. An author cannot compute it and a token cannot hold it. That is the whole argument for trimming it rather than compensating for it: a magic number that is right today is wrong at the next size, the next leading, or on the day the display face is licensed.

What it cost the section header

Section Header is the connective tissue of every page — a mono label, a counter, and a hairline under both — and its rule in components.css has always said that the header owns the air beneath it, so every section on the site opens on the same axis. It declares padding-bottom: var(--space-3), which is 12 px.

Swept at 1280 px across all 38 pattern pages, with each label trimmed to its baseline so the distance could be read off the rule rather than off the box:

Two things were wrong and they are the same thing. The axis was 41 % wider than the token that sets it, and it was two axes, not one — the two sizes on the site differ by 0.69 px, and a third size, or the same label inside a container at another leading, would move it again. A component that inherits both its size and its leading cannot hold an invariant that depends on them.

--space-4 rather than --space-3 is the drawn distance moved onto the space scale, not changed.

16 px is within 0.94 px of what 53 of those headers draw today and within 1.63 px of the fifty-fourth, so the composition the designer signed off does not move; the number under it becomes true, and it is now the same number at every size and every leading. Where the trim is unavailable the padding stays at --space-3 and the drawing is exactly today's — the two branches are 0.94 px apart, and that is the cost of the whole change.

One edge, and which one

text-box-trim takes trim-start, trim-end or trim-both, and text-box-edge says what to trim to. This system trims to cap alphabetic and to nothing else: where a capital starts, and the line it stands on. Those are the two edges the drawing has; text is the font's own ascent and descent, which is the slot itself, and ex is the x-height, which is not where a capital or a Umlaut starts.

The section header trims one edge, the end. The slot above the cap line is air between the header and whatever precedes it, and that air is spent by --section-gap, which is a decision about the page rather than about the component; taking it off would tighten every page's rhythm in order to fix a distance nothing measures. The slot below the baseline is the one the component's own invariant is about. One edge, the one the claim is about.

text-box-edge: cap is legal, means cap text, and draws nothing.

The one-value form fills the missing edge with text — the font's own descent, which is the slot the trim was reached for in order to remove. Beside trim-end it therefore trims nothing at all, and in the file it is one character away from the rule that works. Leaving the property off entirely is worse again: the initial is auto, which defers to whatever over and under metrics the font ships, and the display face of this brand is not licensed yet. scripts/check-cap-line.py fails both.

Everything else in the system is untrimmed on purpose. Trimming is for type that meets an edge the system measures to — a rule, a border, a padding somebody wrote a token for. Prose does not meet one: a paragraph's leading is the drawing, and the slot at its top and bottom is what keeps two paragraphs off each other.

And the block below is lifted, not typed. It is the branch as components.css authors it, comments taken off, written here by the same --fix that writes the table — because a table and a code example one screen apart are the same claim in two registers, and a reader looking for something to paste scrolls past the first to reach the second. It went stale exactly once, on the day .cf-section-header__action joined the trim: the table took the new selector and the example six lines under it went on showing the old rule. 

@supports (text-box-trim: trim-end) {
  .cf-section-header { padding-bottom: var(--space-4); }
  .cf-section-header__label,
  .cf-section-header__count,
  .cf-section-header__action {
    text-box-trim: trim-end;
    text-box-edge: cap alphabetic;
  }
}

Rules

Do

  • Trim where type meets an edge the system measures to — a rule, a border, the padding of a plate. That is where the slot is a lie about a token.
  • State both edges, always: cap alphabetic. One value is a rule that reads as a trim and draws as none.
  • Keep the trim and the padding it changes inside one @supports branch, so a browser gets both halves of the decision or neither.
  • Move the padding onto a rung of the space scale rather than to whatever the slot happened to make. The point is a number the system owns.
  • Re-measure after trimming. The claim is that the drawn distance is the declared one, and that is a fact about a rendered page.

Don't

  • Never trim running prose. The leading between paragraphs is the drawing; the slot at the top and bottom of a block of copy is what holds two blocks apart.
  • No magic number instead. A negative margin that cancels the slot is right at one size, one leading and one font, and this system has three of each.
  • Do not trim an element that carries a background, a border or a focus ring on all four sides — the box shrinks to the cap box and the paint goes with it.
  • Never trim-both to save space. Trimming is for making a stated distance true, not for tightening one nobody stated.
  • Do not assume the slot is half the leading. It is what is left after the engine rounds the baseline to a whole pixel — measure it, do not derive it.
