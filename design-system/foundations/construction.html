The Construction Layer

Every plate in the brand manual shows how it was made. The mark stands inside a measured frame with its unit written on it; the angle grid is a star of rays with the isometric rhombus laid over it; the illustration example carries the angles it was struck at. The website has only ever shipped the finished object. This is the layer that keeps the working — dormant, in the drawing's own coordinates, one rung down the presence ladder — and the reader who wants it asks for it.

A rung that had a name and no drawing

--presence-absent is defined in tokens.css with a comment that names three jobs: “the ghost line: construction geometry, a footprint, a cell that says where a thing stands rather than what it measures.” Until this chapter the rung was spent entirely on a fourth thing the comment does not mention — the not-here-yet register on the arrival band, the progress rail and .cf-line__trace--ghost. The word construction was in the token. The drawing was nowhere.

One figure in the tree had it, welded in. The core on Über uns draws its shells and its axis at --presence-absent and its own source comment calls them “reference geometry”. That is this layer, in one drawing, with no name, no material and no way for a second figure to carry it — which is the difference between a decision and a system.

It matters more here than it would in another brand. A consultancy whose first sentence is Find the answers and whose whole visual argument is contour before fill has one claim to make about itself, and showing the setting-out is that claim drawn rather than written. The manual makes it on every plate. The site made it on none.

The specimen

The system's canonical object — three stacked cuboids, the drawing Geometry & Lines uses to state the whole shape language — with the star it was set out on. At rest it is the object the designer drew. Switched on, it is that object plus its working.

26.57°

45°

What the drawing proves about itself. Two of the eight rays leave the setting-out point and immediately become edges of the object — the top face's upper pair, at 26.57° either side of the datum. The other six run out of the frame carrying nothing at all. That is the isometry stated as a fact rather than as a claim: in a 2:1 system the object can only lie on two of these, and the six it does not lie on are what makes those two mean something.

Markup

Four elements and nothing else: the switch, the plate, the working, the object. The two drawings share a viewBox, which is what makes registration free — inset: 0 over a figure that sizes itself by its own aspect ratio means nothing is positioned twice and the pair cannot come apart at a width nobody tested.

Adding the layer never edits the object. The figure keeps the class, the viewBox and the paths it already had; it gains a wrapper and a sibling. That is the test for whether a drawing may carry construction at all — if the working cannot be drawn beside the object without changing it, the working is not this object's.

Anatomy

Five marks, and four of them are an existing mark of the system taken one rung down the presence ladder rather than a new one invented for this layer.

MarkClassLine typeWhat it says
Ray.cf-construct__ray--presence-absent, 1-4a line the object was set out along. It is not an edge — it runs past the object at both ends.
Cell.cf-construct__cell--presence-absent, 1-4a unit of the lattice, or the frame a mark is measured in. Where the object was cut from.

Measure.cf-construct__measuresolida dimension line and its ticks. The one solid mark here: a rule carrying a word is a label rule, and a measure states something already true rather than provisional.
Label.cf-construct__label—a measure or an angle, and the one part of the layer that is not in the drawing. Layer 6, mono, --text-xs, placed over the plate in the drawing's coordinates by --x / --y.

The switch is .cf-check, whole and unmodified. A real <input type="checkbox"> brings its role, its state, its keyboard behaviour and the system's focus ring with it, so nothing here is reimplemented in ARIA and there is no new control to document.

45°, and the consumer it never had

The manual's Winkelraster plate lists four angles and gives two of them names: Winkel A 63.43°, Winkel B 26.57°, and neutrale Winkel 90° / 45°. All four are tokens. Three of them are everywhere in the tree. --angle-neutral — 45° — had a row in the angle table, a definition in tokens.css, and exactly zero consumers: no stylesheet, no page and no drawing named it.

The angle was not absent; the token was. 45° appears four times in the shipping stylesheets as a literal — inside --iso-transform itself, the checkbox's tick, the radio's rhombus and one act rule — and every one of them is a rotation applied to a mark. Not one is a line struck at 45° across a drawing, which is the only thing the plate uses the angle for.

That is not an oversight, and the fix was never to find something to slant. On the plate the 45° rays are drawn dotted, alongside the solid isometric pair and the lime rhombus laid over them — they are construction, and in a 2:1 system nothing is ever built on them. A system that never draws its construction has nowhere to put the angle. This layer is where it lives, and the specimen above is the token's first consumer.

What it costs, and what it survives

A measure is not drawn

Every label here was an SVG <text> first, and a phone is what that costs. The plate is capped at a rem measure and shrinks to the column, so a 22-unit label in a 780-unit viewBox rendered at 7 px on a 375 frame — below anything this system sets type at, and smaller at every narrower width. SVG text is drawn in the user coordinate system; there is no vector-effect for a font size and no unit that survives the transform.

So a measure is HTML over the plate, positioned in the drawing's own coordinates as a percentage. The mapping needs no arithmetic at runtime: __figure is width: 100% with no height, so it takes the viewBox's aspect ratio and the plate's box is the viewBox's box — --x and --y are (coordinate − viewBox min) / viewBox extent. What that buys beyond the size: the measure takes --text-xs and the mono face like every other label in the system, it grows with the reader's own text size rather than with the drawing, and it is selectable and findable — which on a site named after find-in-page is not a small thing.

The rank is the line type, not the ink

The obvious way to hold construction back from an object is to draw it fainter, and every system that does it that way loses the distinction in the two modes that discard ink strength: forced colours maps authored colour into a palette of its own, and a printer renders a 20 % black as a grey mesh over the copy. The presence ladder is a ladder of dash patterns — “same box, same size, same place”, one rung down — so the working here is drawn in --border-strong, the same contour ink as the object, and told apart by --presence-absent alone.

That decision pays for the fallbacks, and it is worth being precise about which half it pays for. Forced colours does need one line, and not the one this layer expected: the mode does not force SVG paint — the stack has carried a rule saying so since it was drawn — so a stroke authored as --border-strong stays #000 on a dark system canvas and the whole working disappears. It rendered as a mark on nothing, and screenshotting the logo plate in the dark palette is what found it.

What comes through for free is the rank, which is the part that would have been expensive. --presence-absent is a dasharray and the mode does not touch dasharrays, so the moment the ink is restored the construction is still exactly one rung behind the object — a layer held back by opacity would have had to re-argue the whole distinction here, and one held back on paper would have lost it outright. Nothing has to be measured for contrast that was not already measured for the contour it is drawn in. The labels need nothing at all: they are HTML text, and a color is precisely what the mode remaps.

Rules

  • The construction has to be true. Every endpoint in the specimen is a ray clipped to the frame, and the cell is the object's own 2:1 unit — not a length picked to look right. A decorative construction is worse than none: it claims the object was set out one way and it was set out another.
  • It is never the resting state. The designer drew the object, not the object plus its scaffolding. A figure that opens with its working showing has made the working the subject.
  • Under the object, always. Construction is behind what it built, and the occlusion is the evidence: a ray that leaves a corner along an edge was that edge's ray.
  • One rung down, not one shade lighter. Ink strength is not available as a rank here — see above.
  • Only where a reader has asked. The layer is a switch, never a hover and never an idle animation: it costs a paint on demand and nothing at all in between.

Three ways to get it wrong

Don't

  • Draw a ray that stops at the object. A ray that ends where an edge ends is an edge drawn twice.
  • Put construction on a photograph, a chart or a body of type. It is for objects that were constructed; a value measured from data was not.
  • Add a second angle family to make the star look fuller. Four angles, and the drawing is allowed to use fewer.

Do

  • Derive the geometry from the object's own coordinates, then check it: the specimen's rays were solved against the stack's polygon points.
  • Label a measure the drawing cannot show — an angle, a unit, a frame — and nothing the reader can already see.
  • Leave the object's markup alone. If it has to change, the working is not this object's.
