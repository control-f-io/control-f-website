The Share Plate

Every other chapter is about a surface the reader arrives on. This one is about the surface they meet first, somewhere else: the card a pasted link renders as, in a Slack channel, a LinkedIn feed, a Teams chat, a message. Until 2026-09-02 the site shipped no Open Graph metadata at all, so the most-seen brand surface there is was blank — every address unfurled as a bare URL. This chapter is the plate that goes there, the rule for which pages get one, and the one field still waiting on a decision nobody in this repository can make.

Where this comes from

No plate in assets/source/manual/ is about link previews — the format did not exist when a brand manual's last chapter was stationery. So this is derived the way The Sheet is: out of what the manual does establish, and out of the one component that was already built for it. Signet exists because an item sometimes needs a face and has no photograph, and its own launch note named this gap first: the one that is still missing entirely — the plate a shared link renders on LinkedIn.

The plate borrows nothing new. Its ground is the page wash off tokens.css; its grid is the 2:1 lattice from Geometry & Lines; its object is a signet at that lattice's own cell; its light is the single lit face the mark already carries; its logotype is the horizontal lockup at the clear space Logo asks for. Every decision on it was made in another chapter.

A share plate is glanced past, not looked at. It renders somewhere between 400 and 600 px wide in a feed, next to a headline the reader is actually reading. That is the whole design brief: be recognisable at a third of size, in a column of other people's pictures, without saying anything the text beside it already says.

The plate

1200 × 630, the one size every consumer renders — LinkedIn, Slack, Discord, iMessage, WhatsApp, Teams and Facebook all take 1.91:1, and X crops 16:9 out of the middle of it rather than refusing it. Six layers, and they are the six on Materials, minus the one a picture cannot have.

The three above are the live model, drawn by cf-signet.js at the plate's own composition. The shipped plates are the same arrangements rasterised — same seed, same lattice, same ramp, one lit face — with the isometric grid behind them, which a documentation demo has no business faking in CSS.

The plate carries the mark, not the headline

Almost every share card on the web sets the page's title inside the picture. This one does not, and it is a decision rather than an omission.

Every consumer of og:image renders og:title as text immediately beside the image — at the reader's own size, in their own typeface, wrapped by their own client. A plate that also sets the title publishes the same sentence twice, the second time at a size nobody chose and hyphenated by nobody. This site's headlines are German compounds — Flexibilitäts-Asset, Verteilnetz, Musterausschreibung — and a 1200 px plate breaks them badly and silently, in a raster no check can read.

So the plate does the one thing the text beside it cannot: it says whose link this is before a word is read, and it says it differently for every route. Below is the card as it actually arrives, at feed width, with the title where the title belongs.

Why there is a rasteriser in this repository

Every consumer of og:image requires a raster. Not one of them accepts SVG. This repository has no build dependencies and the house rule is that it stays that way, so Signet costed three routes out of that, and this is the one that was taken.

RouteCostTaken
A rasteriser in scripts/ About six hundred lines the system owns forever. No dependency, no runtime, no drift. Yes
The Worker renders it Moves the cost to runtime — and makes a crawler's first fetch a cold render. LinkedIn caches what it gets on that first fetch for weeks, so one cold miss is a card that stays blank. No
A fixed set, exported by hand No machinery at all, and it stops being true the day an eleventh post is published. No

zlib and crc32 are in the standard library, so writing the PNG was the easy half. The other half is a scanline fill with sixteen sub-scanlines a row, exact fractional cover in x, even-odd winding, hairline strokes merged with max() so a corner is not painted twice, one linear gradient, and a flattener for the logo's Bézier outlines. It renders a plate in about a second and a half.

The signet is now drawn twice, and that is the risk this chapter adds to the system. The browser's copy is cf-signet.js; the crawler's is og-plate/signet.py, because a crawler runs no script at all. Two implementations of one model is the drift a design system notices about a year late, with a page and its own share card showing different marks the whole time. scripts/check-signet-parity.py is what stops it: the lattice, the multiset, the ramp and the three faces are compared against the JavaScript and the CSS on every run, and where node is present every arrangement that ships is generated in both languages and diffed. The hash was chosen for this — cf-signet.js says it picked FNV-1a because it gives the same answer in Python.

Which pages get one

A share card is for a link somebody pastes on purpose, which is the same set as the pages that want to be found. So the rule is written out of two things the page already says about itself, and scripts/check-open-graph.py holds it in both directions: 

a page carries Open Graph metadata
EXACTLY WHEN it has a <meta name="description"> and is not `noindex`.
PageCardWhy
The 12 routes with a descriptionYesLanding, Expertise, Über uns, News, a topic, an article, Karriere, an opening, Kontakt, Bewerbung, Datenschutz, Impressum.
404.htmlNoneNo description, and it is served with a 404 status. A polished card over a dead address is a lie.
kontakt-danke, bewerbung-dankeNonenoindex. The page you see after sending something; a link to it is a link to nothing.
suche, suche-leerNonenoindex, follow — and a second argument on top. Their <title> quotes the reader's own query, and og:title would be a fourth place that text is echoed into, on the one surface where the escaping is somebody else's crawler's problem.

The seed is the route, not the file. karriere-leer.html is /karriere with nothing open — the same address in another state — so it carries karriere's mark. Both language editions of a page share one plate too: the mark stands for the thing, not for the language it is read in, and a reader who has seen one edition's card should recognise the other's.

The markup

Thirteen pattern pages carry this as authored markup; build-news.py, build-articles.py and build-stellen.py write it for the topic pages, the reading pages and the openings, whose title and description they own. All four emit it from scripts/og_meta.py, and the check re-derives every line and compares — so the authored copies are provably the same markup as the generated ones rather than merely similar to them.

FieldWhere its value comes from
og:titleThe page's own <title>, verbatim. A card that says something its own page does not is a card that goes stale on the first edit — and it costs no translation, because the string is already in the catalogue.
og:descriptionThe page's own meta[name=description], on the same argument.
og:localeThe edition. build-i18n.py swaps the pair whole, so a page cannot say it is German and that German is its translation.
og:imageAbsolute, where every other address on the page is relative — LinkedIn among others does not resolve a relative one. The origin is SITE_ORIGIN in wrangler.toml, read rather than typed.
og:image:altOne sentence describing what the picture is. A reader who cannot see it is owed that; they are not owed the seed.
twitter:cardThe only twitter:* tag here. X reads Open Graph as its fallback for every other field, so repeating them would be three strings maintained twice — but without this one a large embed degrades to an 80 × 80 thumbnail on Discord and to the small card on X.

og:url is deliberately absent, and it is the one field the domain cutover unblocks. Two public copies of this site are live while the move off Wix is pending — GitHub Pages is canonical and the Worker deployment is noindexed against it. An og:url names one of them, and it would name that one on both; every consumer falls back to the address it actually fetched, which is right on either copy. When wrangler.toml's SITE_ORIGIN goes, this is the line that arrives with it.

What this is one slice of

12 marks ship today, one per route, and the machinery underneath them takes any seed at all. Three surfaces are one loop away and are not built yet, in the order they are worth building:

Do and don't

Do

  • Seed a plate with a path, a slug or an id — something stable that nobody edits for style. Renaming a thing must not redraw its mark.
  • Let og:title and og:description stay the page's own two strings. Writing a third and a fourth is how a card starts describing a page that has since changed.
  • Keep the object and the logotype inside the middle 1200 × 470. Consumers crop, and those are the two things that must survive it.
  • Run build-og-plates.py --check the way you run every other generator's. A plate edited by hand is a plate the next build silently reverts.
  • Read the origin out of wrangler.toml. It is the one place this repository records where the site lives.

Don't

  • Don't set the headline inside the plate. It is already beside it, at a size the reader chose, and a German compound broken by a raster is broken where no check can read it.
  • Don't draw a frame inset from the plate's edge. It is the one graphic that looks broken when a consumer crops it — full-bleed, or nothing.
  • Don't give a noindex page a card, and don't give a card to a page with no description. The two halves of that rule are the page's own words.
  • Don't light two faces to make the lime read at feed size. One lime moment is the rule, and the answer to a small light is a bigger object.
  • Don't let a plate past 300 KB. WhatsApp soft-caps around there and downgrades quietly, on the one surface nobody tests.
  • Don't add a second implementation of anything the plate shares with a page without a parity check beside it. The signet has one for a reason.
