The Cut

Every contour in this system is an edge or a division. A crop is neither, and until this chapter it was drawn as the first — five scroll boxes ending in the same nothing whether a column was hidden behind them or the table simply stopped. The reader was told a table ended when a table was cut.

Where this comes from

No plate in assets/source/manual/ draws a scroll box, because a book has no scroll box to draw. What the manual does establish is the law this chapter applies: Geometry & Lines allows a line to be an edge or a division and calls anything else decoration, and the same plate sets out four line types as a ladder of how present a thing is. Both halves are needed here, and the second is what makes the answer available at all.

The system already reasons about crops in three places and had drawn none of them. check-overflow-clip.py holds a crop to saying it is a crop rather than a scrollport; check-slice-crop.py holds a slice vector to showing the part of the drawing the crop exists for; the print rules release a table's crop because a sheet cannot be dragged. Three gates about what is outside a frame, and nothing that told a reader anything was.

This is the smallest possible claim and it is still a claim. A scrollbar already says a box scrolls, and the five boxes here all show one — none of them hides it, and Rules says never to. What a scrollbar does not say is which edge is hiding something, and on a box scrolled to its end it says the opposite of nothing at all. The mark is one line at one edge, and it is gone the moment that edge is honest.

Three kinds of edge

The system draws boundaries constantly and has, until now, had one drawing for all of them. There are three, and only the first is what a plain hairline means.

Every edge in the system, by what is on the other side of it
EdgeWhat is past itHow it is drawn
A boundaryNothing. The drawing ends.A hairline, solid. The table's last rule, the plate's frame, the footer's top edge.
A cutMore of the same drawing, off-frame and unreachable.Nothing, and correctly: the four process objects run their traces off their crops on purpose, and a mark there would claim the reader could go and look. → Illustration
A crop that scrollsMore of the same drawing, off-frame and one drag away.The mark below. This is the only one of the three where the reader can act on being told.

The distinction that decides the drawing is not is something hidden but can the reader do anything about it. That is why an illustration cut by its frame gets no mark and a table cut by its port does: one is composition, the other is an interface withholding a column.

The mark

One 1 px column at the edge that is hiding something, on --presence-absent — the 1-4 ghost, the rung the presence ladder reserves for a thing that is there and not drawn. Which is exactly what a column past the crop is. It appears at the leading edge, the trailing edge, both, or neither, and neither is the resting state of most boxes on most screens.

Below are two real .cf-table__scroll boxes in narrowed ports. Drag either one, or tab to it and use the arrow keys: the mark crosses from one edge to the other and is gone at both ends of the travel. The second table fits its port and therefore never draws one.

Why this is not a background

The construction every other system reaches for is the scroll shadow: two background layers on the scroll box, one of them background-attachment: local, so the layer that scrolls with the content covers the layer that does not. It is twelve years old, it works in every browser, it needs no script — and it cannot draw this mark. The reason is worth stating, because it is the whole argument for the mechanism that replaced it.

A local layer is positioned against the scrollable area rather than against the box, so the pair can only ever occlude each other. Both states are therefore always painted: the box at the end of its travel does not lose the mark, it gains a second one drawn over the top. Whatever the resting state is made of, it is made of something — and here the resting state has to be nothing, because the alternative is a permanent vertical rule at both ends of every table in the system. Table bans that rule by name: a grid of boxes says spreadsheet; a stack of rules says reading order.

scroll-state(scrollable: …) is a state and not a picture. It asks whether the container can still be scrolled that way, and it is false both at the end of the travel and when there is no travel — which is the case a hand-written scroll listener gets wrong first, because a listener starts at position zero and a box that never scrolls is also at position zero. So a box with nothing hidden draws nothing, and that is not a fallback: it is the same answer the query gives.

A container cannot query itself, and the mark belongs to the box. The query is answered by descendants, so the mark is the box's own ::before and ::after — child boxes of the container, and the only children that can be added to five components without touching a line of their markup. That costs one layout declaration: a pseudo-element in normal flow is a block after the content with no depth of its own, and the mark is the full depth of the crop. A single-column grid gives it that depth as a cell it can stretch in, and gives the two marks the two ends of the content to stand at, which is what position: sticky then measures back from.

The single child is placed in that same cell explicitly, and that is the one thing about this construction that can be got wrong silently: grid auto-placement skips a cell an explicitly placed item already holds, so a second child would land in row 2 — under a mark drawn only to the depth of row 1, rendering as though nothing were wrong. scripts/check-cut-edge.py reads every instance of every cut box in the markup and counts its children for exactly that reason.

The construction

Nothing is added to a page. Every box below is markup that already ships; the whole of the cut is in components.css, behind one gate. 

<!-- unchanged: this is what components/table.html has always shipped -->
<div class="cf-table__scroll" tabindex="0" role="region" aria-labelledby="caption-id">
  <table class="cf-table">…</table>
</div>
@supports (container-type: scroll-state) {
  .cf-table__scroll {
    container-type: scroll-state;
    display: grid;
    grid-template-columns: minmax(min-content, 1fr);
  }
  .cf-table__scroll > * { grid-area: 1 / 1; }

  .cf-table__scroll::before,
  .cf-table__scroll::after {
    content: "";
    grid-area: 1 / 1;
    align-self: stretch;
    position: sticky;
    inline-size: var(--stroke-1);
    background-image: repeating-linear-gradient(
      to bottom,
      var(--border-strong) 0 var(--stroke-1),
      transparent var(--stroke-1) calc(var(--stroke-1) * 5));
    opacity: 0;
  }
  .cf-table__scroll::before { justify-self: start; inset-inline-start: 0; }
  .cf-table__scroll::after  { justify-self: end;   inset-inline-end: 0; }

  @container scroll-state(scrollable: inline-start) {
    .cf-table__scroll::before { opacity: 1; }
  }
  @container scroll-state(scrollable: inline-end) {
    .cf-table__scroll::after { opacity: 1; }
  }
}

The track is minmax(min-content, 1fr) and not 1fr. A table that outgrows its column is the entire subject of this chapter, so the floor has to be the content's own minimum rather than zero — which is the deliberate non-zero floor the track rule credits, and which reproduces exactly what display: block did: the child fills the port, or exceeds it and scrolls. Measured against the same pages before the change, at 375, 768 and 1280: every box, every child and every document identical to the pixel, with one exception — the team strip's <ul> box now measures its own content rather than its port. It paints nothing, so nothing moved.

What every reader gets

There is no half-drawn tier here, and that is a property of the mechanism rather than a piece of luck: the thing the base tier draws is the thing the query draws when it is false.

The scrollbar is the affordance every one of those readers has and it is never taken away. The mark is a second statement on top of it, about which edge and about whether there is anything left in that direction — the two things a scrollbar is worst at on a box a few dozen pixels wider than its port.

Every crop in the system

The roster is not kept here. scripts/check-cut-edge.py reads every rule in the four shipping stylesheets that declares an inline-axis overflow of auto or scroll and takes that as the definition of a crop — the same way the glass budget reads what counts as glass out of the stylesheet rather than out of a list. A sixth scroll box enters this table by existing.

The inline axis: six crops, five marked
BoxWhat it cropsCut

The block frames itself on all four sides and holds --space-4 inside that frame, so a mark stretched across the content box would stand 16 px in from the line the reader reads as the edge: a rule inside the code rather than the edge of it.

The block axis is out of this chapter by argument and not by omission. Three boxes scroll vertically. Two of them — the phone menu's open list and the consent panel — are panels whose whole height the reader chose to open, and a panel is not a crop of a drawing. The third, .cf-blog-grid--port .cf-blog-col, is a genuine block-axis cut: a column of the archive holding more cards than its 36.5 rem port. It wants the same mark on a different construction — the marks lie along the block edges and take their length from the inline axis instead — and it is named here rather than half-covered, which is the state the census is honest in.

Rules

Do

  • Give a new scroll box the cut by adding it to the block in components.css. The gate will ask for it the moment the box exists, and the answer is two selectors, not a component.
  • Keep the box's scrollbar. The mark says which edge and whether there is more; the scrollbar is what says the box scrolls at all, and it is the only one of the two every reader gets.
  • Keep tabindex="0", role="region" and an accessible name on the box. Scrollable content with no focusable child is unreachable by anything but a mouse — WCAG 2.1.1 — and the mark does not change that by one line.
  • Put the box's single child in the grid cell explicitly. One child, one cell; the gate counts them in every page that draws one.
  • Ask whether the crop is one a reader can act on. An illustration cut by its frame gets no mark, and that is the distinction the whole chapter turns on.

Don't

  • Never draw the mark in the resting state. A box with nothing hidden gets nothing — a permanent rule at both ends of every table is the drawing Table bans by name, and on a browser without the query it would be the only thing drawn.
  • No shadow, no fade, no gradient veil over the content. A shadow is not one of the six materials, and a veil hides the column it is announcing.
  • Do not hide the scrollbar to make room for the mark. The mark is an addition to the affordance and never a replacement for it.
  • Never use a scroll listener for this. A listener starts at position zero, and so does a box that does not scroll — the two states it must tell apart are the two it cannot.
  • Do not carry the mark onto paper. A sheet has no travel; a mark there names a column the reader can never reach.
  • Do not put the mark on a second axis by widening this construction. The block-axis cut is a different drawing, and half of one is worse than none.
