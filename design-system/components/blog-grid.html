Anatomy

Three nested blocks and one card. .subdivide owns the tracks; .cf-blog-grid and .cf-blog-axis are two of its .subdivide__row bands and take those tracks through subgrid; the columns and the cards sit inside. Nothing below declares a column count of its own.

The principle

Five columns of equal width, drawn 1, 2, 3, 6 and 6 — the lead card, two columns of cards with a text block, two of single lines. Each column stands for a period: the further right, the older and the terser the presentation. The layout explains how an archive works without a single word.

A column is a year, and the numbers above are how it draws, not how much it holds. On patterns/news.html the newest post is the lead and every post after it stands in the column of its own year; the last column takes every year older than the four that fit, which is why its tick is a range. A year that runs long is not paged away — the column scrolls. See The port.

The port

.cf-blog-grid--port is the archive's own form of this grid: a fixed height of 36.5rem, and each column its own scroll. It is worn on patterns/news.html and the topic pages, and deliberately not on the Weiterlesen grid under an article — three cards have nothing to scroll and a port there is a fixed height with air under it.

  • 584 px is twice what the grid drew. Measured at 1440 and 1920: 293.66 px, six compact rows stretched to 48.6 each. Twice that is 587.3, and 584 is the number on the 4 px unit under it.
  • Not a whole number of rows, on purpose. A compact row is 40.59 px of its own accord at every width this grid opens at. The port's two hairlines take 2 px, and the 582 left hold fourteen rows and 14.7 px of the fifteenth — a year with more posts than fit always shows one cut off by the bottom edge. On a static page that cut row is the only thing that says “keep going” before anyone has touched anything.
  • The rows are the cards' own heights. --rank's equal 1fr rows are what fills a content-sized column; in a port they would divide 584 px by six and give each single-line headline 96 px of box to float in. So the port sets grid-auto-rows: min-content and align-content: start, and a thin year leaves air at the bottom of its column — which is what a thin year looks like. The lead is the exception: one card, one 1fr row, and its picture absorbs the height.
  • No tabindex on the columns, and that is the rule rather than an oversight. A scrollable region needs to be keyboard-operable; one whose content is focusable already is, because tabbing between the cards scrolls it. Every card in these columns is a link or a listing next to one.
  • Folded, there is no port. Below the fold the columns stack and the height goes back to auto: a fixed-height box nested inside the page's own scroll is the pattern that takes a thumb hostage. Both fold widths are base.css's — 44rem for the primitive, 56rem for the five-column form.

The title picture

A post names one picture that stands for it — bild: in its file, the Titelbild property in Notion — and the card is drawn from it: .cf-blog-card--media on the card, an <img> with .cf-blog-card__image as its first child. scripts/build-news.py writes both, and the same call writes the Weiterlesen cards under an article, so the two cannot drift apart.

  • Optional, and the layout does not reserve a hole for it. A card without a picture is the card this grid has always drawn. An archive where half the posts carry a photograph reads as an archive; a row of grey placeholders reads as a fault.
  • The picture pays the padding. A card with one drops its own 12 px box padding and pays it on the title and the meta line instead, so the photograph meets the cell's hairline on three sides. That is what makes the left of the grid read as a wall of pictures rather than as framed stamps.
  • It takes the slack. flex: 1 1 auto with min-height: 0: in the tall lead cell the picture grows and the headline sits under it, instead of space-between spreading three items down a column of air. aspect-ratio: 3 / 2 is the basis, object-fit: cover the crop.
  • Columns 4 and 5 carry none. Those rows are 2rem high. A picture in one is a 48 px stamp painted from a file sized for the 1008 px reading plate — the waste scripts/check-image-scale.py exists to catch, and bytes a reader on a train pays for something nobody can read. Terser to the right is the layout's whole argument; the picture is the first thing to go, not the last.
  • alt="", always. The picture sits inside the link whose text is the headline. A description of the photograph makes a screen reader read every card twice, so there is no caption field on the post either — the title is the text alternative, and the <img> steps out of the way.
  • Its own dimensions on the tag. width and height come from the file's header, so the box is reserved before the bytes arrive — eighteen cards on one page is eighteen chances to move the grid under the reader. loading="lazy" and decoding="async" for the same page.

The entry with nothing behind it

The archive lists what has been published. scripts/build-articles.py writes a page for every post that carries text, and build-news.py draws the rest — an entry the catalogue knows about and has no article for — as a <span> with .cf-blog-card--listing instead of an <a>. The last row of the last column above is one. 

<span class="cf-blog-card cf-blog-card--compact cf-blog-card--listing">
  <span class="cf-blog-card__title">Wie Stadtwerke zu Energiedienstleistern werden</span>
</span>
  • Every rule of the card is written for the class, not for <a>. So the row keeps its geometry, its hairline, its type and its place in the column's row count. The grid does not develop a gap where an article is missing.
  • What it drops is the light. .cf-blog-card--listing sets background-image: none, so the sheen that travels across a card on hover does not run here. That light is the affordance and there is nowhere for this one to arrive; a row that lights up and does nothing is the worse failure.
  • No href, no tabindex, no disabled state. It is not a control that has been switched off — it is text. A keyboard passes over it, which is correct: there is nothing to activate. Nothing needs aria-disabled, because nothing here has a role to disable.
  • It is a modifier, not a fourth kind of card. It combines with --compact, --lead and --media, and the generator applies it at whatever rank the entry falls at.
This variant ships and had never been drawn. It exists in components.css and build-news.py writes it, and until this specimen no page in design-system/ carried the name at all — so a reader could not learn the variant exists, and the only way to see one was for a post to lose its text. scripts/check-markup-answered.py holds a modifier that ships under patterns/ to a live specimen on its component's page; this one escaped it by never shipping, because today every post has an article. It is one unwritten post from shipping, which is not the same as not existing.

Behaviour

  • Below 896 px of container — 56rem, a viewport of about 1006 on a page that is one .container wide — the grid collapses to one column and the timeline is dropped. It is a container threshold, not a viewport one: the same grid is readable in 896 px of page and unreadable in 896 px of viewport behind a 272 px documentation sidebar, and only the container can tell those apart. Binary-searched on patterns/news.html: folded at 1006, open at 1007.
  • The five-column form carries .subdivide--late, always. The primitive's own fold at 44rem is the number for three columns; five columns of a 44 rem container give each single-line title 116 px of text, and the widest first words in this grid — “Smart-Home-Systeme”, “Erneuerbaren-Ausbau” — need 120 or more, so two of the twelve tail headlines rendered as a fragment of one word plus an ellipsis. base.css carries the measurement, scripts/check-subdivide-late.py holds every five-column call site to the modifier. → foundations/layout.html
  • Single-line titles are cropped with text-overflow: ellipsis, and the crop is visual only. The headline stays whole in the document, so it is the link's whole accessible name, it is what the search index reads, and it is what a screen reader announces. → The crop
  • Hover lightens the cell (white 45 %); nothing moves.
  • Every cell with an article behind it is an <a> — the whole area is clickable, not just the title. → The entry with nothing behind it
The timeline has no tracks of its own. The ruled grid and the axis under it are two .subdivide__row bands of one .subdivide, so the axis adopts the grid's columns through subgrid and cannot drift from them. They used to be siblings, each declaring five equal columns independently — which held only because both happened to say five. patterns/blog-artikel.html already runs the same grid at --subdivide-count:3; adding an axis there would have put five ticks under three columns. → foundations/layout.html

A tick stands on the leading rule of the column it names, which is where the application plate draws it. writing-mode: vertical-rl starts its text at the block-start edge — the right — so a stretched tick landed on the trailing rule and read as labelling the column next to it. justify-self: start is what puts it back.
The timeline is decorative. It is aria-hidden because the years already appear in each article's metadata. Without that, a screen reader announces “Aktuell 2026 2025 2024 2022–2023” with no context at all.

The crop, and the attribute this page used to promise

A compact row is one line and most headlines in this language are longer than a fifth of the grid, so the tail columns crop. This page used to end that sentence with “— the title attribute carries the full text.” No title attribute is written anywhere in this system. Not in this demo, not on the archive, and not by scripts/build-news.py, which draws every card that ships. The sentence had been describing a mechanism that did not exist, which is worse than describing none: it reads as the accessibility question already answered.

It is not being added, and the promise is what goes. The title attribute has no keyboard mechanism in any browser — there is no way to make its tooltip appear without a pointer — so it hands the full headline to exactly the readers who least need it and to none of the ones who do. It is not shown on touch either. And where a screen reader is set to read it, it reads the row's text and then reads the attribute, so every cropped headline is announced twice. An attribute that helps nobody and talks over somebody is not an improvement on nothing.

  • Nothing is missing from the machine-readable page. CSS truncation crops the paint, not the document: the headline is the link's accessible name in full, it is what scripts/build-search-index.py indexes, and it is what is copied when the row is selected. The ellipsis exists only on the screen.
  • The reader it does cost is the sighted one with a pointer, and that is a real cost this component pays on purpose. Current practice is blunt about it: do not crop an interactive element's own text, because a link whose visible words are a fragment is a link nobody can weigh. The tail columns break that rule knowingly — being terser to the right is the layout's whole argument, and the mockup draws it — so the rule is paid off rather than waved away: only the two tail columns crop, the newest posts are never a fragment, and the entry is one click from its own page and reachable whole through the search and through its topic. What is not acceptable is buying that knowingly and then telling the reader it was free.
  • Cropping harder is the failure to watch for. white-space: nowrap is unconditional, so a narrow column crops more, and folded to one column on a phone the rows are as wide as the page and crop least. The width to check is not the phone — it is the five open columns at the narrowest viewport they survive, which is the 1007 that .subdivide--late exists to hold, and it is why the fold is at 56rem and not the primitive's 44rem.
If a headline has to be readable in full on the page, it does not belong in the tail. The rank is the decision, not the type size: move the entry to a column with a rank of 3 or less and it gets two lines and a meta row. There is no “compact card that wraps” — that is a three-row column drawn at six rows' width, and it takes the port's row arithmetic with it.

Do and don't

Do

  • Build it on .subdivide and let the axis take the grid's tracks. One declaration of the column count, in one place.
  • Carry .subdivide--late on every five-column form, always.
  • Give a card that has a picture .cf-blog-card--media and put the <img> first, with alt="" and the file's own width and height.
  • Draw an entry with no article as a <span> with .cf-blog-card--listing, not as a link to nowhere.
  • Let a thin year leave air at the bottom of its column. That is what a thin year looks like.
  • Keep one tick per column and write the oldest one as a range when it holds more than a year.

Don't

  • Don't add a title attribute to a cropped headline. It is pointer-only, and it makes a screen reader read the row twice. → The crop
  • Don't put a picture in a tail column. A 2 rem row paints a 48 px stamp from a file sized for the reading plate, and the reader pays for the bytes.
  • Don't describe the photograph in alt. The headline is the link's text; a description makes every card read twice.
  • Don't wear --port where there is nothing to scroll. Three cards under an article in a fixed-height box is a box with air in it.
  • Don't give the columns tabindex. Their content is focusable, so tabbing between the cards already scrolls them.
  • Don't spend lime in a cell. The archive is a list of links, not the screen's one light moment.
  • Don't page the grid. A year does not end because a page does — that is what the port replaced.
