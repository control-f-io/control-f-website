Components

Signet

A mark that stands for one thing and is drawn from that thing's own name. Nine plots of the isometric lattice, blocks standing on some of them, and one face carrying the light. The same name always draws the same mark; two names never draw the same one by accident.

Nothing in it is a new shape. The object is the one on Geometry & Lines · Isometric objects — the brand's standard building block, stacked cuboids on the 2:1 lattice, top faces filled, 1 px contours, exactly one element carrying the lime gradient. What the name decides is only where the blocks stand and how high, which is the one degree of freedom that object already had.

The set

Ten marks, seeded with the ten post slugs in content/news/. One of them carries the light; the rest are ink. That ratio is the component's default and the reason for it is below.

They are siblings and none of them is a variation on another. That is what a mark generated inside a construction system buys over one generated inside a hash: the family resemblance is not a style applied afterwards, it is the only thing the generator can draw.

Construction

The frame is a 120 × 120 viewBox. The lattice cell is 36 × 18 — 2:1, so every ground edge is 26.57° and every vertical is 90°, and no angle is ever typed into the generator. A storey is 18 px, which is twice the cell's half-height and is what makes each block a cube rather than a slab. Plot (a, b) of the 3 × 3 field has its centre at 

x = 60 + (a - b) * 18
y = 69 + (a + b) * 9

and the field is painted in order of a + b, back to front, so a near tower covers a far one and the mark reads as depth rather than as pattern. Every coordinate the generator emits is an integer.

The alphabet is finite, and its density is fixed

The nine heights are not nine independent draws. They are a permutation of one fixed multiset — 

0 0 0 1 1 2 2 3 3

— so every signet in the set has exactly three empty plots, exactly two towers at full height, and exactly the same quantity of ink. The name chooses the arrangement and nothing else. That is 9! / (3! 2! 2! 2!) = 7560 distinct marks, and it is the reason the set has no outliers: there is no name that draws an empty field and none that draws a solid block.

This is the part a hash-into-free-parameters generator cannot promise. The identicon convention — a symmetrical pixel grid in a hashed hue — is deterministic and unique and belongs to no brand at all, and its density swings from nearly blank to nearly solid across the set. Fixing the multiset moves the variation into arrangement, where the construction system can still govern it. The number above is computed from the multiset at run time by CFSignet.count, so it cannot drift from the line it describes.

The name becomes an arrangement

FNV-1a over the name gives 32 bits; an xorshift32 seeded from those bits drives a Fisher–Yates shuffle of the nine heights. Both are chosen for being short, table-free and identical in Python — which will matter on the day the share plates are written by a build script rather than by the browser. The same name gives the same arrangement in every engine, on every machine, for as long as the name exists; nothing is stored.

The one lime moment, and why it is not the default

Of the two full-height towers the light goes on the frontmost — largest a + b, ties to the larger a — and never on both. One lime moment per mark, which is the manual's rule about a screen applied to an object.

But it is still possible to put twenty lit signets on one screen, and that is the one place in this system where an object could break the rule twenty times over without any single mark being wrong. So the ink form is what the generator emits unless it is asked otherwise. --ink paints the peak in the same tone as every other top face; the light is opt-in, once, on the mark that is the screen's lime moment.

The lit face reads as Glas, and that is the source material, not a mistake

The gradient is the family's — lime → Glas → CF-Grau on the near rake, 63.43°, with the #DBFC60 waypoint at 19 % of the lime leg that every drawing in this system carries. Its axis is the designer's own, scaled: on the stack in Geometry the lit rhombus is 240 px wide and its gradient runs (80, 160) from a point 50 left and 80 above the centre. This rhombus is 36 px wide, so both are multiplied by 0.15 and land on (12, 24) from (−7.5, −12).

Projected onto that axis, the face spans t = 0.225 to t = 0.824 — which is past the lime stop at both ends. Lime itself never lands on the face; what lands on it is the leg just after lime, and by the opposite corner it is Glas. That is true of the designer's stack in exactly the same proportion, because the axis was scaled rather than re-placed, and it is what the manual means by light as a temporary effect rather than a fill: the lime is off the edge of the surface and what you see is the fall-off. Re-anchoring the axis to the face would put a flat lime lozenge on a 36 px rhombus and break the family in one object.

The stops are written as attributes and not as classes. scripts/check-gradient-family.py walks every gradient the site ships and recomputes the waypoint's offset from the leg's own ends and its colour from the oklab path. It reads the SVG. A stop whose colour lives in a stylesheet is a stop it cannot see, and a gradient it cannot see is a gradient that drifts.

Three sizes, and what the smallest one drops

An empty plot is drawn and not omitted: the field is nine plots whether or not anything stands on them, and a plot with nothing on it is exactly the presence ladder's faint rung — the 1-2 line, this system's word for a thing that is there and is not the subject. Leaving it out would make the mark a silhouette; drawing it solid would make nine towers of which three happen to be flat.

At --sm that line stops being a line. 3 rem is 48 px, the frame scales by 0.4, and a 2-on-4 dash lands at roughly one device pixel on and two off — which does not read as an outline, it reads as dirt on the mark. So --sm drops the empty plots and the four construction dots with them, and keeps what survives at that size: the blocks, the contour, and the light. Set --signet-size below 4 rem by hand and you own that decision yourself.

The markup

There are two ways to put a signet on a page and they are not equivalent.

1. The mark is in the HTML

Paste the SVG. It is in the markup, it is in the first paint, and it survives cf-signet.js being blocked, cached stale or switched off. This is the only correct form for a mark a crawler has to see — a share plate above all, because the crawler runs no script at all. Print it for any name here:

Naming it, and when it is not a picture

    

      A signet is decorative wherever the thing it stands for is named beside it
      — a card whose title is right there, a row in a list. There the mark is
      aria-hidden, which is what the generator emits when no label is passed, and
      a screen reader hears the title once instead of twice.
    

    

      Pass label only where the mark is the item's face and no text
      repeats it. Then it is role="img" with an accessible name, and the name is
      the item's title in words — never the seed, and never "signet" or "generated mark", which
      describe the drawing rather than the thing.
    

    

      It carries no motion and needs no prefers-reduced-motion branch: the mark is
      still. Under forced-colors the three face greys would go out together, so the
      mark collapses to what it is under the paint — a contour drawing on the canvas, with the
      lit face left as the one filled shape, so the moment survives a mode that has no lime in it.
    

  


  

    
Where this is meant to go, and the one thing in front of it

    

      This component exists because of a gap that has nothing to do with news cards.
      The site ships no Open Graph metadata at all — no og:title,
      no og:description, no og:image, no Twitter card, on any of the
      eighteen pattern pages in either edition. Every link to control-f.de posted into LinkedIn,
      Slack, Teams or a message renders today as a bare address. For a company whose readers
      pass its writing to each other, that is the most-seen brand surface there is, and it is
      blank.
    

    

      The signet is the plate for it, and the four surfaces beneath it are the same asset at
      other sizes:
    

    

      
SurfaceSeedSizeState

      
        
Share plate — og:imageThe page's own path--lg, on a 1200 × 630 plateBlocked. See below.

        
An article with no bild:The post's slug--lgReady. Dormant — all ten posts currently carry a picture.

        
A topic page's headerThe topic's slug--lgReady.

        
A search hitThe hit's address--smReady. Wants a designer's eye on a results list first.

      
    

    

      The share plate needs one decision this system cannot make for itself.
      Every consumer of og:image — LinkedIn, Slack, iMessage, WhatsApp — requires a
      raster. SVG is not accepted by any of them. This repository has no build dependencies and
      the house rule is that it stays that way, so there are three routes and each costs
      something:
      


      1. A Python rasteriser in scripts/. zlib is in
      the standard library, so writing the PNG is the easy half; the hard half is filling and
      anti-aliasing nine polygons and a gradient, which is a few hundred lines of code the
      system would then own forever. No dependency, real weight.
      


      2. The Worker renders it. There is already a Cloudflare Worker in
      worker/. A route that draws the signet and returns a PNG keeps the repository
      dependency-free and moves the cost to runtime and to a cache. It also means a crawler's
      first fetch is a cold render.
      


      3. A fixed set, exported by hand. Twenty plates for twenty routes, drawn
      once in a vector editor from the generator's output. No machinery at all, and it stops
      being true the day an eleventh post is published.
      


      The metadata itself is not blocked by any of this.
      og:title, og:description, og:type,
      og:url, og:locale and its alternate, og:site_name
      and the Twitter equivalents are text, they are already written on every page as
      <title> and meta[name=description], and
      build-i18n.py already flips a page's language pair. That half should land
      first and does not need the answer above.
    

  


  

    
Rules

    

      

        
Do

        
    
          
  • Seed it with something stable and unique — a slug, a path, an id. That is what makes the mark an identity rather than a decoration.
    • 
          
  • Paste the SVG on any page a crawler reads. The script is for documentation and for lists the page does not know in advance.
    • 
          
  • Use the ink form by default, and light exactly one mark per screen.
    • 
          
  • Leave it aria-hidden wherever the item's title stands beside it.
    • 
          
  • Reach for --sm in a list and --lg where the mark is the page's own face.
    • 
        

      

      

        
Don't

        
    
          
  • Never seed it with a title, a date or anything a person may edit. Renaming the thing must not redraw its mark.
    • 
          
  • Don't put a signet where a photograph exists. It is the answer to no picture, not a second picture.
    • 
          
  • Don't light two on one screen, and don't light one on a screen that already has its lime moment.
    • 
          
  • Don't set --signet-size below 4 rem without dropping the plots — --sm does it for you.
    • 
          
  • Don't add heights to the multiset to get "more variety". Density is a property of the system; 7560 arrangements is not the constraint you think it is.
    • 
          
  • Don't animate it. It is a mark, not an illustration — the moving objects in this system are on Motion.
