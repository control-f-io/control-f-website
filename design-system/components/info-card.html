Every part is optional except the card. A count with no facts, a title with a paragraph and nothing else, facts with one column — all of them are the same component with fewer children, because the spacing is declared on the parts rather than between them.

The material is a modifier

The base card is layout, type and a plain surface. .cf-info-card--glass replaces that surface with the material, and it is a modifier rather than the default for two reasons that turned out to be the same reason.

Glass exists to calm a complex graphic beneath it, never to sit on a flat surface. That is the material's own rule, in one line, from Materials. Add --glass to a card that stands on the ground, on an illustration, on anything the reader can still see through it. Add it over a flat --surface-page and it is only a grey box — and the base card, which has a border, is what you wanted.
And the budget has to be legible from the markup. The first version of this component was glass unconditionally, and Expertise turned the material off again in its own stylesheet for the four cards that did not need it. check-glass-budget.py counted that as six blurred layers on a two-layer page, and it was right twice over: it reads the HTML rather than resolving the cascade, because a census with an unstated exception in it is worse than no census — and a class that says I am glass with a page-local rule saying except here is the drift these scripts exist to stop. Carry the modifier where the material is spent.

Bearing glass, and its floor

Text sits directly on this card, so it takes --surface-glass — the bearing tint, whose 46 % white is the floor at which black type still clears 4.5:1 composited over a pure black backdrop. The measurement and the reason that value has almost no room to move are both in tokens.css, section 9. Veil glass is the other tint and it is not this: it carries nothing but opaque objects.

Measured on the shipping card, over the lattice at stage size: the plate composites to rgb(234, 234, 236), and its darkest single pixel — a blurred lattice line — to rgb(228, 229, 230). The five stops of the ink foil land between 6.47:1 and 10.54:1 against that worst pixel, which is better than the 5.24:1 the class guarantees on CF-Grau, because glass is lighter than CF-Grau.

There is no fallback to write. When the browser cannot blur, or the reader has asked for less transparency, tokens.css redefines --surface-glass to the opaque stand-in and the card goes to a solid plate with its contrast intact.

Count your blurred layers

A page may carry one of these frosted. The system allows exactly two blurred layers on a page and the navigation band is always one of them. That is a measured budget, not a stylistic one — see the note on --glass-blur. Anywhere a second card is wanted on the same screen, the second one is not glass.

Expertise is the case that already came up, and its answer is worth copying. Four cards stacked in one grid area would have been four blurred layers, so the material is spent once — on a single empty .cf-info-card--glass under the copy column — and the four cards hand over on top of it as plain cards, giving up only their own opaque back so the plate shows through. One lectern, changing copy, which is also the better drawing: a lectern does not change when the page on it does. Below the enhancement threshold the plate is display: none and the four are ordinary bordered cards, so the census counts what is actually drawn on either path.

The title takes the foil

.text-foil--ink on the title is what puts colour in the card, and it is authored on the element rather than baked into the component. That is deliberate: the one-foil-moment-per-screen budget is a fact about the screen, and a component cannot know how many of itself are on one. The card styles the title; the page decides whether it iridesces.

Ink, never the lit half. This surface is light. .text-foil on its own is the light-on-dark ramp and lands between 1.1:1 and 1.5:1 here. .text-foil--ink is the one that belongs on glass.

Rules

  • --glass on a graphic, never on a flat surface. The material's own rule, and the one that decides whether the modifier belongs.
  • One --glass per page. The navigation band is the other blurred layer, and check-glass-budget.py counts the modifier off the markup. A second card on the same page is a plain card.
  • Ink foil on the title, at most one per screen. Never .text-foil on its own here — this surface is light.
  • Glass is not outlined. The modifier's perimeter is --glass-border, a lit rim, and its top edge carries the same one pixel of Weiss → Glas → Sky that .cf-nav::after and .cf-btn--glass end at. A black line round a glass plate is the first thing anybody sees.
  • The question is ruled, not boxed. A second box inside a card is a card inside a card.
  • The chips are the card's. They are not a global chip and should not become one until a component that is not this needs them.

Where it is used

  • Expertise — four of them, one per field, handing over on a single hoisted plate as the track scrolls.
  • Nowhere else yet. The scroll study at prototypes/expertise-scroll.html carries the same track and the same build, but not this card: it is a study about pacing, and giving it a second glass surface would have been a third blurred layer for nothing.
