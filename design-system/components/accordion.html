Components

Accordion / FAQ

Full width, hairlines as the only boundary, arrow on the right. Built on <details> — it works without JavaScript and is keyboard operable without us doing anything for it. What the stylesheet adds is a lit row on hover, a panel that slides rather than jumps where the browser can do it, and nothing else.

Default

Four of the eleven questions that ship on Expertise, at the answer length this page recommends. The counter says four because four stand under it — see Section Header.

SPS und Leitsysteme über OPC UA und Modbus, Historians, MES- und ERP-Systeme, Cloud-Telemetrie sowie Dateiablagen. Wenn es ein Protokoll hat, bekommen wir es angebunden.

Ja – das ist eher der Normalfall als die Ausnahme. Anlagen laufen 20 Jahre, Software selten. Wir bauen die Brücke, statt einen Austausch zu verlangen.

Hosting wahlweise in Ihrem Rechenzentrum, bei STACKIT oder Hetzner – in jedem Fall in der EU. Verschlüsselung in Transit und at Rest, rollenbasierte Zugriffe, nachvollziehbare Audit-Logs.

States

Four, and until now this page named none of them. Three are drawn by the stylesheet and one by base.css; the difference matters, because the one that is not the component's own is the only one that is a conformance requirement. All four are states of a screen; a sheet has no closed row at all, and that is a clause of the print law rather than a fifth state.

StateWhat draws it
Closed The row, the hairline under it, the arrow pointing right. The resting state of every entry but at most one.
Hover --sheen-panel travelling across the row. The gradient sits on the summary at background-size: 200% 100%, parked in its own transparent half, and hover slides background-position to 100% 0 over --duration-slow (480 ms). Light crossing the row, not a tint on it.
Focus The same sweep, plus the system's one focus ring — --stroke-2 solid --focus-ring at 2 px offset. The sweep is not the indicator; see the note below.
Open [open] on the <details>. The arrow rotates 90° over --duration-base (240 ms) and the panel comes down — instantly, or over 480 ms where the browser can interpolate. See Opening.
The sweep is not a focus indicator, and for a long time it was doing that job alone. base.css carries the system's single focus ring, and its selector names five tags plus [tabindex] — a list of the things a person thinks of as a control rather than of the things the browser gives focus to. <summary> is natively focusable, is none of the five and carries no tabindex, so it never received that ring. Measured on a shipping FAQ row at 1440 × 900, unfocused against focused: the sweep alone is 1.23:1, the browser's own fallback outline 13.61:1, this system's ring 14.91:1. WCAG 2.4.11 asks 3:1 of a focus indicator, so the sweep was never one — the whole job was being done by a UA outline nothing in this repository had asked for, at 1 px where the system draws 2 and in the UA's near-black rather than in --focus-ring, which is the token that flips to lime on an inverse surface. <summary> is in that selector now, and scripts/check-focus-ring.py reads the selector and the markup rather than trusting the next hand-kept list.
Not glass. These rows sit on the page wash, and frosted glass over a flat surface is only a grey box. A light crossing the row is the honest version of the same gesture, and it costs no backdrop-filter — which is the most expensive declaration in the stylesheet and is budgeted per page. → Materials

Opening

A panel that jumps to its height gives the reader no thread from the row they clicked to the text that answered it. The panel slides instead — where the browser can interpolate to auto, and nowhere else.

PartWhat it does
interpolate-size: allow-keywords On .cf-accordion__item. Lets block-size animate to the intrinsic keyword instead of snapping to it. Scoped to the item rather than to :root, so it does not quietly change every other auto-sized transition in the system.
::details-content The panel box. block-size: 0 closed, auto when [open], transitioned over --duration-slow with overflow-y: clip so the text is cropped rather than made to overflow while the box travels.
content-visibility, allow-discrete Transitioned alongside it. Without the discrete transition the content is hidden at the first frame of the close and the box animates down over nothing.
Two gates, and the fallback is the current behaviour rather than a degraded one. The whole block sits inside @supports (interpolate-size: allow-keywords), which is Chromium-only as of 2026 — Firefox and Safari never enter it, and the panel opens exactly as <details> has always opened, which is instantly and correctly. Inside that, @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference): a reader who asked for less motion gets the instant open too. That second gate is deliberate and not merely the global duration collapse — tokens.css does drop every duration to 1 ms under reduce, but a 1 ms interpolation to auto is still a layout animation, and the honest answer to that request is not to run one.

On paper

Every panel is open, and until now none of them was. A closed <details> is not laid out faintly on a sheet — it is not laid out. Printed through Chromium at A4, the three sets above came out as a stack of questions: measured under emulated print media, eleven panels on Expertise, checkVisibility() false on all eleven. The reader was holding the only part of that page they printed it for, with the answers missing.

It had gone unseen for the reason print faults do: every screenshot this repository takes is screen, and on screen the component is correct. It was also being actively protected — .cf-accordion__item has been on the may-not-be-cut register since that register existed, so the sheet was keeping whole an object carrying a question and no answer.

On paperWhy
The panel is open content-visibility: visible on ::details-content, with block-size and overflow-y given back from the opening animation — that block sets both on a closed panel, at the same weight, so this one has to be later in the cascade than it is.
The arrow is gone It is the control that opens the panel and it draws which way the panel stands. With every panel open and only one carrying [open], it would print the wrong state beside an answer already standing under it. → the withdrawn register
The row is one column A grid gutter is drawn between tracks whether or not the second one holds anything. Left alone, the question would wrap 24 px short of the answer under it. 
@media print {
  .cf-accordion__summary .cf-arrow { display: none; }

  .cf-accordion__item::details-content {
    content-visibility: visible;
    block-size: auto;
    overflow-y: visible;
  }
  .cf-accordion__summary { grid-template-columns: minmax(0, 1fr); }
}
::details-content is what makes this a stylesheet's job at all. The panel is a box the user agent generates and hides, and before that pseudo-element was exposed there was no author selector for it — which is why a printed accordion on the web is normally either a beforeprint script or nothing. It is Baseline as of September 2025 (Chrome 131, Firefox 143, Safari 18.4). This component already stands on it for the opening, so the clause introduces no floor the accordion did not already have, and below that floor the sheet is what it was.

The rules live at the foot of components.css with the two registers rather than beside the component, because that is the end of the last stylesheet loaded and a print rule that loses at equal specificity is a comment. The law and its third clause are in Print; scripts/check-print-law.py holds every panel a <details> closes to it, derived from the stylesheet rather than from a list.

Accessibility

  • <details>/<summary> brings role, keyboard operation and state with it. Enter and Space on the focused summary toggle the panel; the summary is the row's only tab stop, and Tab from there moves into the open panel's own links. No ARIA, no script.
  • The summary carries the system's focus ring because base.css names it. That is a fix, not a given — see the note under States.
  • The arrow is aria-hidden; the element communicates its own state.
  • The default marker is removed (list-style: none plus ::-webkit-details-marker) — the CF arrow replaces it visually.
  • At most one open on load, and that should be the most common question.
No heading element inside the <summary>. It is tempting — HTML's content model for <summary> allows one element of heading content, and a FAQ of eleven questions is exactly the thing a screen-reader reader would like to skim by heading. It does not work. <summary> maps to a button role, and a button's children are treated as presentational, so the heading semantics are dropped — inconsistently across engines, which is worse than dropped everywhere. Adding role="button" to force the matter drops them in macOS Safari too. The markup would look like an improvement in the source and be one nowhere.
Name the set instead. The APG's Accordion pattern does ask for a heading per header, and it asks it of a set of scripted buttons with aria-expanded — a different component from this one, which is a set of disclosures. What this component can have, and what all three shipping sets do have, is a <section> around it named by aria-labelledby pointing at the section header's <h2>. That heading is what a reader navigating by heading lands on, and it names the whole set rather than one row of it. It is also why the copy-paste block above starts at the section and not at .cf-accordion.
FAQPage JSON-LD is no longer worth writing, and this page used to say it was. The instruction here was "ship FAQPage JSON-LD alongside it for rich results". Google narrowed FAQ rich results to well-known authoritative government and health sites in August 2023, and deprecated them outright on 7 May 2026 — they no longer appear in search results for anyone, and the reporting around them was withdrawn over the two months after. The schema.org type is still valid and existing markup does no harm, so nothing needs removing; but writing it new for a company FAQ buys nothing a reader or a search engine can see. No page in this system ever shipped it, and that is now the correct state rather than an omission.

Rules

Do

  • Phrase questions in the customer's words, not your own.
  • Keep answers short: three to five lines, then link out.
  • Six to eight entries in a set you are writing now — beyond that it becomes documentation.
  • Wrap the set in a <section> named by the section header's heading.
  • Make the header's counter count what stands under it, when it is a quantity rather than a position.

Don't

  • No accordions for content everyone is supposed to read.
  • No nested accordions.
  • Don't auto-close the other entries — it costs more than it gives.
  • No heading element inside the <summary>.
  • Don't reach for glass here. The rows stand on the wash; the sheen is the state.
