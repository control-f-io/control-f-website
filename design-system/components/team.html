Components

Team

Three forms for the same people. The grid on the Über uns page shows which people; the group shot closes that section; the strip is the compact form for a page that only has to say there are people behind the technology. All three read the same .cf-person typography, so a name and a role are set the same way whichever form carries them.

No shipping page carries the strip today. It was drawn for the landing page, which is where the mockup places it, and patterns/landing-page.html does not — nor does any other page. The four-person specimen above is the form's only live instance, and the blog byline is the other reader of its 144 px derivative. It is kept because the mockup draws it, the derivative is in use, and a page that needs to say "there are people here" in 120 px should get this and not a second drawing of the same cell.
The wrapper is not decoration. A scroll box with no focusable child cannot be scrolled by keyboard — the reader tabs straight past it and never sees the people it cut off. Measured on a 1440 layout at 375 px the strip overflows by 585 px and at 768 px by 192 px, so that is two of the four widths this system is tested at. tabindex="0" makes the box a tab stop and arrow-scrollable; role="region" plus a name from the section heading says what the reader has landed in. It is the same contract .cf-table__scroll already carries.

The role="region" has to sit on a wrapper rather than on the <ul>, because a role replaces the element's own — a labelled region would have cost the list its count.
It is not a carousel. The APG carousel pattern is for content that rotates: it wants aria-roledescription="carousel", a slide group per item, previous and next buttons and a rotation switch. Nothing here rotates. A list that is wider than the screen is a list, and it gets list markup — which is also what the APG recommends for this case.

Anatomy

PartElementWhat it does
.cf-team-gridulThe ruled field, on .tiles with a 21rem tile and no gap. It draws the top and left hairline; each cell draws its own right and bottom, so the field's outline is exactly as large as its cells. Names itself as a container (cf-team) so the cell can read the column count.
.cf-team-grid__itemliOne cell. Spans four row tracks of the parent as a subgrid, so a name that wraps grows the track for the whole row. The inset is a ratio — 15 % of the cell on each side, leaving 70 % for the portrait, which is what the mockup's 48 px in a 294 px cell comes to — and is carried as margins on the four lines rather than as padding, for a Chromium reason written over the rule.
.cf-team-grid__photoimgThe 640 × 800 studio portrait at 4 : 5, object-fit: cover, --radius-sm on the corners because the designer's vector draws rx="2" on every one. alt="" — the name is the next line.
.cf-person__namepGeist SemiBold at --text-lg. Not a heading: the section has one heading, and seven names under it are items, not sections.
.cf-person__rolepGeist Mono Medium at --text-xs, uppercase, --text-secondary. On the German page every title is English and carries lang="en" — on this element and never on the name beside it.
.cf-person__focuspOne sentence on what the person works on, in the page's language, at --text-md over a --border-default hairline. Prose, not chips. Grid only — the strip has no room for a third register.

No scroll box here: the field wraps rather than overflowing, so there is nothing to reach past. The cell count is what moves — see the note on the track minimum in components.css.

Accessibility

  • Both the strip and the grid are a <ul> of <li>: a team is a countable set of people, and the count is announced on entry. role="list" is on it because list-style: none removes the role in Safari.
  • The strip's scroll box is a tab stop. A scrollable element has to be reachable by keyboard — either focusable itself or holding something that is — and a cell here holds no link, so the box carries tabindex="0". Arrow keys then scroll it. Firefox has focused a scroller with no focusable children on its own for years and Chromium has since version 132; that is a fallback in two engines, and it gives the stop no name. role="region" with aria-labelledby pointing at the section heading does, so a screen reader lands in "Das Team, region" and not in an unnamed group.
  • The region role sits on the wrapper and never on the list: a role replaces the element's own, and a labelled region in place of the <ul> would have cost the list its count.
  • The focus ring is the system's, drawn inward on the strip: the box is full-bleed, and at the ring's usual 2 px outward offset two of its four sides sat off the screen and read as the hairlines having thickened. This is the system's answer whenever a ring has nowhere outside to be drawn, and the strip is no longer the only place it is needed — the archive's port crops its cards the same way, not with the viewport but with a scrollport its own component declares. The rule to carry is about the crop and not about the screen.
  • The grid has no tab stop and needs none — nothing in it scrolls and nothing in a cell is a link. Contact goes through the contact page, which is why the rules below keep LinkedIn icons out of the cell.
  • alt="" on every portrait, because the name is the adjacent text; a descriptive alt on the group shot, because there is none. See the note under Group shot.
  • Names are paragraphs, not headings. The section heading is the one heading, and seven items under it are list items — a heading per person would put seven h3s into the outline of a page that has nothing under any of them.
  • English job titles inside the German page carry lang="en" on the role line and on nothing else (WCAG 3.1.2). The focus sentence is German like the rest of the page, and the English edition is generated from it.
  • Contrast: the name is full ink; the role and the focus line are --text-secondary, which reads 5.9 : 1 on the page wash. The grayscale on the strip's avatar is a filter and touches no text.
  • Nothing here moves, so there is nothing for prefers-reduced-motion to stop. Under forced colours the hairlines are borders and survive as they are.

alt="" on portraits is deliberate. The name sits right next to the image in text — an alt would only duplicate it. A photo that stands alone, without an accompanying name, gets a descriptive alternative text — the group shot above is that case.
Seven people, and the divisor rule was written for six. The track minimum in components.css was chosen so that the column count always divides the head count — 3, 2 or 1 for six people, never a ragged last row. The shipping grid has seven now, so at three columns the last row is one cell and at two it is one cell again; no minimum can fix that for a prime count, and the field's stepped outline is the correct drawing of it. The specimen on this page keeps six so that it still demonstrates the rule. What settles this properly is the open question below — a strip has no last row.
Open against the source material: the Über uns mockup draws a strip, not a grid. Measured off assets/source/mockups/ueber-uns.jpg, the team block there is full-bleed with 294 px cells that run past the right edge — 4.9 of them fit a 1440 frame, so the fifth is cut, exactly the way the landing-page strip is drawn. The implementation renders it as a contained field that wraps instead. The wrapping grid is the better answer for a ten-person page and it is what ships today, but it is an improvement over the material rather than the material, and no decision has been recorded for it. A designer settles which one the page keeps.

What could be taken from the plate without settling that question is the proportion, and it now has been. In the mockup the portrait is inset 48 px from the cell's top and both sides — 205 px of photo in a 294 px cell, 70 %. That 70 % is the decision; 48 px is only what it came to at the designer's cell width. So the inset is written as the ratio — padding: 15%, resolved against the grid area — and the portrait is 70.0 % of the cell at every column count the field reaches, from the 426.3 px cell of the 1440 frame down to the 332.8 px cell of a 375 px phone. It was previously a fixed --space-8, which landed 69.5 % at the one cell width the grid then had and would land 85.0 % at today's — next door to the 84.5 % the 16 px inline inset used to draw, a portrait with no cell left around it.

The cell is now wider than the mockup's rather than narrower. The track minimum used to be pinned low to keep all six people on one row at 1280, which is what held the cell at 213 px and made the mockup's 294 unreachable. Three columns and two rows lifts it to 426.3. The rule the old minimum was chosen for — a ruled field only looks right when the column count divides the six — is kept: this minimum produces 3, 2 and 1 and never the ragged 4 or 5.

Do

  • Shoot everyone in the same setting — one session, one light.
  • File every frame under the person in it, and derive the 144 px avatar from the same frame the grid draws — the 440 px square on the head, columns 100 to 540 and rows 60 to 500 of the 640 × 800 portrait — so both forms show the same face and the avatar is the tight head crop the photography table asks for.
  • Keep role titles short, consistently in one language, and the same on every page — the record is content/autoren.json.
  • Remove people who have left promptly.
  • Name the strip's scroll box after the section heading it sits under.

Don't

  • Never put a real name beside a face that is not that person's — not on the site, and not in a specimen. A placeholder gets a placeholder name.
  • No placeholder avatars among real portraits.
  • No mixed treatments — either all b/w or all colour.
  • No LinkedIn icons in the cell; contact goes through the contact page.
  • Never ship the strip without its scroll box — a bare .cf-team-strip__list hides everyone past the fold from a keyboard.
  • Don't caption the group shot. The header already says who is in it.
