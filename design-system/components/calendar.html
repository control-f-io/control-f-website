Components

Calendar

One month, and nothing that is not one month. Seven columns of numerals with a hairline under each week, no cell boxes and nothing vertical — and one lit day, the one the reader is standing on. It is the table read on two axes and the found state read over time, and it introduces no drawing of its own.

Default

A caption naming the month, a head row of weekday labels on the strong hairline, and a week on each subtle one. A day that has something on it is a link and stands on the found state's ground line; today carries the light. Every other day is a numeral and nothing else.

The caption is the month. Not a heading above the grid — the same rule the table states, for the same three reasons: a <caption> is the table's accessible name, it is announced when a screen reader lands on it, and it stays attached when the block is moved or printed. If the page also wants the month set large, that is a section header above the component, and the caption still says September 2026.
The specimen's today is fixed, and a served page's is not. A file cannot know the day it is opened, so this month lights 17 September and will go on lighting it. aria-current="date" is a claim about the day the page is served and it is the server's to write — the same standing every state in Arrival & Progress has, and the same one the news archive has for its paging.

The three rungs

The brand is named after find-in-page. A month is a page of days, and the day the reader is standing on is the match they are standing on — so the drawing for a day is the drawing the found state already settled, at numeral scale. Three rungs, one figure, and an object moves along them and never across: same numeral, same place, same size.

RungMeansDrawnMarkup
Unmarked A day with nothing on it The numeral. No rule, no box, no fill. <span class="cf-calendar__day">
Contour A day something stands on The numeral on its ground line — --found-rule at --stroke-2, --found-offset clear of the descenders. <a class="cf-calendar__day" href="…">
Contour + light Today The same numeral with --found-light behind it. either, plus aria-current="date"

Today always takes the rule as well as the light, including a today with nothing on it — because the fill is not what carries the mark. Lime on CF-Grau is 1.37:1 and on the white end of the page wash 1.13:1; it cannot hold a boundary at either end, at any opacity. The ground line holds it, at 13.5:1. That is the found state's own argument and it discharges never carry a state by colour alone in the same stroke: the rule makes the mark, the fill ranks it. And a day with nothing on it is not an empty day when the reader is standing on it.

The light is spent once, by arithmetic rather than by a rule. One lime moment per screen is a rule about composition that somebody has to keep. A month has exactly one today, so a calendar with four marked days composes one lit numeral and three contoured ones whatever anybody does to it — the same way a result set with twelve matches has one current one. → Colour · Lime is light
A link is what makes a mark. There is no class for “something stands on this day”: the contour is declared on a.cf-calendar__day and nothing else, so a day cannot be marked unless there is somewhere to go. That is structural rather than conventional, and it is what keeps the drawing off a day the reader can do nothing with.

The week column

Optional, and nothing turns it on but the markup: a calendar that carries the column has eight cells to a row and one that does not has seven. A modifier here would be a class saying what the markup already says.

It is a <th scope="row"> and not a first cell, because it names what the rest of the row is about — the table's own test. It departs from that component's row head in exactly one property: a row head there is set in the body face because it is content, and KW 37 is not content, it is a figure. Every figure in this system is mono. The German business week is the unit maintenance windows and delivery dates are named in, and it is ISO 8601's week, not the calendar's own count.

What stands on a day

Not the cell. A 44 px slot cannot hold a title, and a filled chip in a grid is the fill this brand spends contour instead of — the same objection the table makes to zebra striping. So the grid is an index: it says which days have something on them and where in the month they fall, and the register underneath says what.

That register is not a new component. .cf-events is the third consumer of the one a search result and an open position already share — a stack of type blocks standing on hairlines, a mono line, a linked title, a couple of sentences. It adds three words to seven selector lists and not one declaration.

An event's facts go in the mono line, not in a <dl>. The vacancy's four facts — Standort, Anstellung, Umfang, Start — are a description list because there are four of them and they are named. An event has two, when and where, and both are short enough to read as a strip. The <time> carries the machine-readable value; a time of day goes in the datetime as well as in the words.

When seven targets do not fit

Seven slots at 2.75 rem come to 19.25 rem, eight to 22 — more than a 320 px viewport has between its gutters. Below that the component does what the table does: it scrolls, in the same box, with the same three attributes. Nothing is dropped, nothing is folded, and the slot never goes under the touch floor.

What gives the table its minimum width is the slot itself. Every cell carries min-width: var(--calendar-slot), so auto table layout resolves every column's min-content to the same figure and the columns come out equal without table-layout: fixed being asked for. It is the table's own mechanism — a floor the browser cannot compress past — reached by a different property, because a table's floor is a word that must not wrap and a calendar's is a fingertip.

The three attributes are not optional decoration. tabindex="0" makes the box a tab stop and arrow-scrollable, role="region" gives it a landmark, and aria-labelledby pointed at the caption's id gives that landmark its name — so the month is named once and the region borrows it. Without them the last two weekdays do not exist for anyone not holding a mouse. SC 2.1.1 Keyboard, level A. → Table · When it is wider than its column

There is no card-per-day transformation and no agenda view at small widths. A month is a shape read at a glance, and a stack of thirty rows is not that shape; the agenda already exists on the page as the register, at every width.

Leaving the month

The grid draws no days of the neighbouring month, so it needs a way out, and the component does not draw one. Stepping between months is the page's navigation and pagination is already that shape — a rule, a status line that says where you are, and the two steps on the right. The same don't the table writes about sorting and paging: that is a server's work and a different pattern.

Where it sits is the page's call, and one thing follows from it: a step placed directly under the register takes .cf-pagination--flush, because the register's last row already draws that hairline and two of them 0 px apart read as 2 px. A step placed under the grid keeps its own rule — the calendar's last week is ruled --border-subtle, which is not the same line. → Pagination

Why the neighbouring month is not drawn. A cell holding 31 in a table captioned September contradicts its own caption, and the only way to say this one belongs to another month is to grey it. --text-muted is #919191, which is 2.0:1 on CF-Grau — the one step this system does not have, and the reason four other components carry the same note. Weight and size are worse: both change the drawing rather than its rank. The cells before the first and after the last are therefore empty <td>s, which keeps every row seven cells wide and every column under its own weekday.

On a dark surface

No modifier. Every colour in the component is a semantic token, so the calendar inverts by being put somewhere inverted — data-theme="inverse", the footer, or the consent dialog. --found-rule is rebound by that theme and the ground line inverts with it; --found-light is not, because the light is the light on any surface, and black on lime is 18.5:1 wherever it lands.

One custom property, --calendar-slot, and it is not a new number: 2.75 rem is .cf-pagination__page's floor, which is .cf-nav__toggle's floor, which is WCAG 2.5.5 and Apple's HIG. It is written once because four declarations read it, and four copies of a floor are four floors.

Accessibility

  • It is a real <table>, and that is the accessibility decision the rest of the component is built on. A month is genuinely tabular — the column is a weekday, the row is a week — and scope is what says so with no script. A display: grid or role="grid" version of this would drop the row and column semantics and then need JavaScript to put them back.
  • Every header cell carries an explicit scope. Without it the association has to be guessed from position, and a calendar read out as thirty unlabelled numbers is a calendar with no weekdays in it.
  • The weekday head is spelled out for the ear, and stays two letters for the eye. Mo is a legible abbreviation for a reader looking at the grid and an ambiguous syllable for one hearing it. It has to stay two letters: measured in the browser, seven full weekday names at 11 px with white-space: nowrap put the grid's floor at 359 px against the two-letter head's 308, and a 375 px phone leaves 334 px between its gutters — so the abbreviated month fits that screen and the spelled-out one scrolls it. The head therefore carries aria-label="Montag": one attribute, no second element, and no hidden twin of the word to drift from the visible one. <abbr title> is not the answer — announcing an abbreviation's expansion is off by default in the major screen readers. Same for KW. A <th> is not a user interface component, so SC 2.5.3 Label in Name does not apply; nothing here is a target anybody names out loud.
  • A day link's name is its numeral, and its purpose comes from the table. SC 2.4.4 Link Purpose (In Context), level A, names the cell's own row and column headers as context, and this component supplies all three: the caption says the month, the column head says the weekday, the row head says the week. An aria-label restating the date would be a second copy of the date, and it would drift.
  • The slot is 2.75 rem in both axes at every width, including inside the scroll box. WCAG 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) asks 24 px; this is the system's own floor and clears 2.5.5 as well.
  • No state is carried by colour alone. Today is a fill and a rule, a marked day is a rule, and both are announced independently — aria-current="date" for the first, being a link at all for the second.
  • Contrast, measured against CF-Grau — the dark end of the page wash, so these are floors and every one of them improves as the wash lightens: the numerals and the weekday head 13.5:1, the caption and the week number 5.9:1, the ground line 13.5:1, and today's ink on the lime 18.5:1. On the inverse surface the same four are 21:1, 10.6:1, 10.6:1 and, unchanged, 18.5:1 — black on lime is black on lime wherever the lime lands. The week rule is a separator rather than a UI contour — the structure is carried by the markup, not by the hairline — so it is not load-bearing for the 3:1 floor.
  • The scroll box is focusable, has role="region", and takes its name from the caption. SC 2.1.1, level A. It is a tab stop even on a wide screen where it does not scroll, which is the same honest cost the table pays and the same answer: one redundant tab stop beats two unreachable weekdays.
  • In print the light is dropped and the rule stays. On paper there is no reader to be standing on a day, so the current rung has nothing to rank against, and lime prints as a pale wash over the ink it is meant to be behind. Every marked day prints as the contour, which is the rung that was carrying the meaning anyway — the found state prints the same way and for the same reason.
  • Under forced colours today takes Mark/MarkText, and the pair was never the fault — the box was. It is the system pair for exactly this meaning and it is the pair <mark>, ::target-text and both ::highlight() names already take, so the calendar takes it too rather than drawing today differently from every other match in the system. What this component recorded for a while, and deferred to base.css “to all five rungs at once”, was that the pair does not paint under the dark scheme. Measured again in Chromium 151, both schemes, this page and the found state served and screenshotted: an inline <mark> paints correctly in both palettes and always did, and this <time> did not. Chromium lays an opaque Canvas-coloured backplate behind each run of text in this mode, and that backplate belongs to the block container that lays the text out. A mark inline in a paragraph is not that container, so its plate survives; this <time> is a flex item of an inline-flex day, so it is its own block container and the backplate went down on top of its plate — white in the light scheme, where the ink still read, and black in the dark one, where it did not. forced-color-adjust: none stops the backplate without leaving the reader's palette, since every value in the rule is a system colour keyword and still resolves to the theme. The lit day is now a yellow plate with a black numeral and a black rule in both schemes, verified by render. What was deferred to base.css was one declaration, not a change of pair; the two element rungs there carry the same escape, and the three highlight pseudo-elements remain the half nothing reaches — which costs this component nothing, because a day is an element. Nothing was lost meanwhile that was not announced anyway: aria-current="date" is unaffected by a colour palette.

Rules

Do

  • Caption every calendar with its month and year, and point the scroll box's aria-labelledby at it.
  • Wrap it in the scroll box with all three attributes, every time — including when you are sure it fits.
  • Mark a day only when there is somewhere to go, and let the link be what marks it.
  • Put the register under the grid and give every entry the id the day links to.
  • Let the server write aria-current="date". It is a claim about the day the page is served.
  • Start the week on Monday and number it to ISO 8601 if you number it at all.

Don't

  • No vertical rules and no cell boxes. The week rule is the whole drawing, the same as in the table.
  • No shaded weekend and no shaded month. A column fill is a fill doing a hairline's job, and a vertical rule is decoration.
  • No days of the neighbouring month. There is no grey in this system that can say what they are.
  • No dots, no badges, no counts in a cell. The mark says something; the register says what.
  • Don't spend a second lime moment. The light belongs to today and to nothing else on the screen.
  • Don't build a date picker out of it. <input type="date"> is the platform's own, it is keyboard- and screen-reader-complete, and this system does not spend a shipping script on a convenience.
