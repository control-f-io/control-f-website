Components

Page Header

The masthead of a subpage: a display title, a 1 px page line under it, and one mono row split left and right beneath that. Sixteen of the site's eighteen routes open with it — every page except the landing page, which opens with a hero instead, and 404, which opens with its own case. It is the most widely shipped component in the system and, until this page, the only one with no page of its own.

Default

Three parts and no options. The title says where you are, the rule closes it, and the mono row underneath carries what the page holds on the left and the aside about it on the right. Nothing here is a link and nothing here moves.

Kontakt

Konstanz am Bodensee Antwort in der Regel innerhalb eines Werktages

The title in every demo on this page is a <p>, and on the site it is the <h1>. A documentation page has one <h1> and it is the component's name at the top of this document; a second one inside a specimen would put two page titles in the outline of a page that has one subject. .cf-page-header__title is presentation only — it sets the face, the size, the leading and the tracking, and it carries no heading semantics of its own — so the specimen is the drawing and the markup block is the element. Colour does the same substitution with the same title, for the same reason.

One item in the mono row

The row is a split, so the right-hand item takes an ink step down to --text-secondary — it is the aside, not the statement. A row with one item has no right-hand side, and that item stays in full ink.

It read the other way round until this page was written. The rule was .cf-page-header__meta :last-child, and with one child the only item is both first and last — so it took the quiet ink. That is not hypothetical: the News archive's row is generated, and build-news.py writes “the post count and, when there is more than one page, which page this is”, so the archive drops to one span whenever the register fits on a single page. Measured at 1440 × 900: patterns/news.html's lone span computed rgb(72,72,72) while the first of two on patterns/ueber-uns.html computed rgb(0,0,0) — same component, same slot, two inks, decided by how many posts happen to exist. The selector is :last-child:not(:first-child) now, which says in the stylesheet what the paragraph above says in prose.

The title wraps to as many lines as it needs and the rule follows it down. There is no truncation and no line cap: a headline that runs to three lines is a long headline, and cutting it would leave the reader with a page whose subject they cannot read.

The object on the rule

One page carries a drawing in its header — Über uns, from the designer's mockup. It is not a decoration placed near the rule: the rule is its axis. The figure is a child of .cf-page-header__rule, centred on it with top: 50% and translate: 0 -50%, so the drawing's own dotted axis and the page line are collinear by construction at every width, with nothing to keep in sync by hand.

The rule then stops where the drawing starts and resumes where it ends. In the mockup the page line and the object's axis are one line that changes character as it passes through the drawing — solid rule, the object's own 1-4 axis across the gap, solid rule again. A line ruled straight through the nucleus reads as lying on the object rather than as its axis, so the rule opens at the same two stops the figure occupies: 27.1485 % and 72.8515 % of the content column.

The drawing is not reproduced on this page, on purpose. It is the designer's vector verbatim — every path in the source file's own coordinate window, with no placement transform wrapped around it, so there is nothing to retype and nothing to drift. A second copy here would be a fork of a 60-line figure that only one page ships, and this directory has a check for exactly that habit (check-illustration-source.py). It lives on patterns/ueber-uns.html, and scripts/check-figure-clearance.py holds every number in the table above at every width and both scrollbar widths.

The room the reach buys is empty on both sides of the drawing, and on Über uns that emptiness is most of what a reader sees below the fold — the section under the header is a pinned track, and a pinned track at scroll 0 has drawn nothing yet. Scroll Cue stands in that air. The header places it and does not draw it: .cf-page-header__cue sets the band's floor and one horizontal position — 55.03 % of the column, the centre of the gap the axis already crosses between the shells and the reflector, which is the one part of the composition drawn empty on purpose.

Which titles take the foil

Nine of the sixteen headers set their title in .text-foil.text-foil--ink and seven do not, and the split is not arbitrary — it is one rule nobody had written down:

  • A title that names a destination takes the foil. Über uns, Expertise, News, Karriere, Kontakt, Suche, Bewerbung. These are the words the nav bar uses; the page is the place, and the foil is what the place looks like.
  • A title that is a document or a sentence does not. Datenschutzerklärung, Impressum, a blog headline, a job advert, “Ihre Nachricht ist angekommen.” The foil is an emphasis, and an emphasis spread across a three-line sentence stops being one. These are set in flat ink.
An open tension, recorded rather than resolved. The README's own budget is that .text-foil stays one headline per page, and the footer's “Jetzt Projekt starten!” is a foil headline on all eighteen pages. So a page whose title takes the foil spends two. That may well be right — the two are a page apart and never in one viewport — but it is a decision the budget does not currently make, and reading the site is how you learn it. Named here so the next person to touch either end knows the other exists.

    </div>
  </div>
</header>

It is the first child of <main>, above every section on the page. The section directly under it takes .section--tight: the header has already spent its bottom padding, and a full --section-gap on top of that is two gaps where the design has one. → Layout & Grid

Two pages give the header id="anfang" — Über uns and Expertise, the two with no hero above it. That is where the act rail's “to the top” jump lands, and it is the same scroll-margin-top the hero takes on the pages that have one: one rule for the top of a page, whichever of the two elements opens it.

Where it goes

  • On every page that is not the front door. Sixteen routes carry it: Über uns, Expertise, News, a topic listing, a blog article, Karriere, a job advert, Bewerbung, Kontakt, both confirmations, Suche, both empty states, Datenschutz and Impressum.
  • Not on the landing page. That page opens with .cf-hero — a full-height opening with a video behind it. A page has one opening.
  • Not on 404. The error page draws its own case, deliberately: it is not a page of the site, it is what the server says when there is none, and giving it the site's masthead would dress an absence as a destination.
  • Not for a section inside a page. That is Section Header — a label, a counter and a hairline, at <h2>. The page header is the page's own title and there is one of it.

Accessibility

  • It is a <header> and it is not a banner. The APG's landmark guidance is explicit that a header element is not a banner landmark when it descends from article, aside, main, nav or section. This one is the first child of <main>, so it contributes no landmark and the page keeps the one banner it should have — the site's nav bar. Moving it above <main> would give every page two banners.
  • The title is the page's only <h1>. Everything under it starts at <h2>. The class carries no semantics, so the element has to be right in the markup — see the note under the first demo.
  • The rule survives forced colours now, and did not before. It is a 1 px box painted with background, and forced-colors mode discards background-color and replaces a gradient background-image with none. Measured in Chromium's forced-colors emulation at 1440 × 900 before the fix: on patterns/news.html the plain rule computed rgb(255,255,255) — Canvas, on Canvas — and on patterns/ueber-uns.html the interrupted one computed rgba(255,255,255,0) with its gradient gone. The page line was absent on all sixteen pages, in both schemes. It is redrawn as a border-block-start in CanvasText, and the gap is kept by drawing the two lit ends on the rule's own ::before and ::after at the same two stops — because .cf-iso's forced colours block keeps the drawing on the page, and a solid line through it is the one read the gap exists to prevent. Below 56.25 rem the figure is hidden and the segments give way to the continuous border again.
  • Contrast. The second mono item is --text-secondary on the page wash: 5.9:1 against CF-Grau at the top of the page, rising as the wash travels to white. The title is full ink or the foil, whose worst measured sample on this component is 5.45:1 at 375 px. Both clear AA for their size with room.
  • The figure is aria-hidden="true". It is a drawing of nothing nameable — reference geometry, an axis, two shells and a dome — and there is no sentence about it that a reader of the page would be missing. It also takes pointer-events: none, so it cannot swallow a click aimed at the text it sits behind.
  • Nothing in it is focusable, and nothing in it moves. The header has no controls, no script and no animation; the only interactive thing that ever sits inside it is the breadcrumb, which brings its own focus behaviour. There is therefore nothing here for prefers-reduced-motion to switch off.
  • The mono row is one <p>, not a list. Two spans separated by justify-content: space-between read as one line in the accessibility tree, in source order — left item first, which is the order they are read in on screen. A list would announce a count that means nothing.

Rules

Do

  • Open every subpage with it, as the first child of <main>, and give the section under it .section--tight.
  • Put the page's subject on the left of the mono row and the aside on the right — a place, a count, a date, a reading time.
  • Give a title that names a destination the foil, and a title that is a document or a sentence flat ink.
  • Hang a figure on the rule rather than beside it, and let the rule open for it.
  • Wrap a long title. The rule follows it down and the header grows.

Don't

  • Don't put a button, a filter or a search field in it. It is a title, not a toolbar — actions belong to the section that owns them.
  • Don't put a third item in the mono row. It is a split, and a third item has nowhere to be.
  • Don't set the title with anything but <h1>, and don't add a second <h1> further down.
  • Don't add a subtitle paragraph under the rule. The mono row is the only line the shape has, and a lead sentence belongs to the first section.
  • Don't use it on the landing page or on 404 — both have an opening of their own.
