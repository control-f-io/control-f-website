The title carries the page's foil.

.cf-footer__title is set in .text-foil — the cool half of the spectrum, clipped into the letterforms. The footer is the right place for it: it is black, where the foil clears 11.9:1 at its worst sample, and the lime on this screen has already been spent on the button beside it. It is the only light-on-dark foil headline in the system; the shadow half, .text-foil--ink, carries the same material on light surfaces, and the rule is one foil moment per screen rather than per page.

The designer's mockup paints this line solid near-white, so this is a documented deviation rather than a transcription — see the README. It is also exactly what the unenhanced fallback renders, because .cf-footer__title sets no colour of its own and inherits white from data-theme="inverse". A browser that cannot clip a background into text therefore lands on the mockup's own value.

The footer mark

Three isometric planes with the CF symbol on the top one. The planes use exactly the three line types of the shape language — solid, 2-1, 1-4 — and fade downwards. The image says in one graphic what the company does: several layers of data become one answer at the top.

The mark is purely decorative (alt="") — the wordmark sits underneath it in the meta row.

The one modifier: --detached

The footer has exactly one modifier, and until now this page did not mention it while two of the site's pages shipped it. .cf-footer--detached adds margin-top: var(--section-gap-sm) — the footer supplying the air above itself, instead of the last section supplying it below. 

<section class="section section--flush">…</section>
<footer class="cf-footer cf-footer--detached" data-theme="inverse">…</footer>
It is half of a biconditional, never a decoration.

The last section is .section--flush if and only if the footer is --detached. --flush gives up the section's bottom padding and --detached puts the same distance back, so the two together make one gap. Use one without the other and the page is wrong in whichever direction you broke it: both, and patterns/kontakt.html stood its footer off at 240 px where every other page measures 120; neither, and the footer would sit straight against the last row of content.

Change one, check the other — and change the distance in tokens.css, never on the page. The edges either modifier touches are set out in full on Layout → Sections, and the reason this edge reads --section-gap-sm rather than --section-gap is under footer clearance: the footer is not a second section of content but a black plate that has already ended the page, and it spends 96 px of its own before the CTA.

Two pages ship it — patterns/ueber-uns.html and patterns/blog-artikel.html. Every other page ends on a normal .section and takes a plain .cf-footer.

Imprint and privacy policy are legally required and must be reachable in one click from every page (§ 5 DDG, Art. 13 GDPR). That is why they live in the footer rather than the main navigation — but they are there on every page. Cookie-Einstellungen sits in the same row for the same reason: withdrawing consent has to be as easy as giving it (Art. 7(3) GDPR), so it reopens the settings dialog from wherever the visitor happens to be. It is a <button> dressed as a link, because it opens a dialog rather than navigating — see Consent.

Accessibility

  • The link row is a <nav> landmark with a name. It is the one group of links that stands on every page of the site, and it holds the two routes the law requires — Datenschutz and Impressum. Until it was wrapped it was a bare list inside contentinfo: reachable by tabbing to the end of the document, and not offered at all to a reader moving by landmarks, which is how a screen-reader user gets to the foot of a page without walking it. The label is Seiten und Rechtliches — what the row holds. Not “Navigation”, which the role already says and which the APG names as the mistake to avoid; the breadcrumb settled the same question the same way.
  • Two navigation landmarks on a page, two different names. The bar at the top is Hauptnavigation and this is Seiten und Rechtliches, so a landmark list offers them as two entries rather than one word twice. check-a11y.py fails on two landmarks sharing a role and a name.
  • A button inside a navigation landmark, deliberately. Cookie-Einstellungen is not a link and does not become one for tidiness — it opens a dialog. It stays in the row because withdrawing consent has to be as easy to find as the privacy policy beside it (Art. 7(3) GDPR), and a landmark that groups links is not spoiled by the one control that belongs with them.
  • The separator is drawn and not spoken. content: "·" / "" renders the dot and hands the accessibility tree the empty string, so the row does not read “Über uns dot News dot Karriere”. It is declared twice — a browser without the / alt syntax drops that declaration and keeps the plain one, so the drawing degrades to the drawing.
  • Target size passes WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 by the spacing exception, not by size. The links are 11 px mono with no padding: a 13 px box. What clears the criterion is the distance between them — measured in Chromium at 375, 768, 1280 and 1920 px, the closest two target centres are 28.1 px apart (Expertise and Karriere, on two wrapped lines at 375), against the 24 px the exception asks for. It is the narrow viewport that is tightest, and it has 4 px in hand. Anything that shrinks the row's --space-3 gap or its 12 px separator margins spends that margin — which is why the row is not the place to save vertical space.
  • And that is why the footer is not held to the bar's 44 px floor. .cf-nav__link takes min-height: 2.75rem in the folded bar because it is the primary way to reach another page on a phone; check-touch-floor.py enforces it there. The footer row is the secondary route to the same pages, read last, and it earns its pass on spacing — the breadcrumb is the third answer again, at 28 px of padded target. Three rows of 11 px mono, three different arguments, all of them written down.
  • Contrast on the black plate: the meta row is --text-inverse-dim, 62 % white, which composites to rgb(158, 158, 158) at 7.84:1. Hover and focus take it to --grey-000 at 21:1. The title's foil clears 11.9:1 at its worst sample.
  • Hover and focus are one rule, so the underline that answers a pointer answers a keyboard identically — link and button together, at a 0.25em underline offset. The focus ring is the system's; data-theme="inverse" redeclares --focus-ring to --cf-lime, so it is lime on black here rather than black on grey.
  • The wordmark is a link and says so. Its <img> carries alt="Control-F" — the link's whole accessible name, since there is no text in it. The mark above it carries alt="" for the opposite reason: the same name twice on one plate is one name too many.
  • Printed, the plate is dropped and the ink is forced to --cf-schwarz — the title, the meta row and an outline CTA each by their own rule, because each names a colour the theme scope would otherwise answer in white on white paper.

Rules

Do

  • Exactly one CTA. The footer is a closing, not a second menu.
  • Set data-theme="inverse" — contours and the focus ring flip with it.
  • The sentence above the button is a prompt, not a heading.
  • Pair --detached with .section--flush on the last section, or use neither.
  • Keep the link row inside its named <nav>. It is the only landmark that offers the two legally required routes.

Don't

  • No four-column sitemap.
  • No newsletter form beside the CTA — one or the other.
  • No social icon gallery; text links are enough.
  • No mail address and no phone number. The form is the channel, the Impressum is the address, and check-footer-parity.py fails on either link.
  • Don't tighten the link row's gaps. They are what carries the target-size criterion — see Accessibility.
