Components

Notice

One message standing in the flow of the page: a qualification inside an article, a warning above a form, the sentence a failed submission comes back with. A hairline box with a 4 px band on its leading edge, and the band's line type is the tone. Nothing moves, nothing floats, nothing disappears on its own.

Three tones

Hinweis qualifies something — read it or don't. Achtung says something will go wrong if you don't. Fehler says it already has. There is no fourth, and in particular there is no success tone; below says why.

Hinweis

Die Taktzeiten in der Tabelle sind Richtwerte aus eigenen Projekten, keine Norm.

Achtung

Diese Stelle ist seit dem 12. August besetzt. Bewerbungen darauf werden nicht mehr gelesen.

Fehler

Ihre Nachricht konnte nicht übermittelt werden. Nichts davon ist verloren — das Formular steht noch so, wie Sie es ausgefüllt haben.

Why the tone is a line, not a fill

Every notice component in the field carries its severity in a fill. USWDS paints a green, a yellow and a red band across the box; Carbon tints the surface; GOV.UK fills the header. None of that is available here. tokens.css calls --feedback-error “the one colour outside the brand palette, and the only one” — so there is no green to be successful in and no amber to warn in, and inventing them would cost the palette more than this component is worth. A fill would also lose the argument twice over: the system draws with contours, and a filled panel is the grey slab that Materials spends a chapter arguing against.

The brand already has an axis for exactly this. The four line types are catalogued twice in the manual — once as a specimen strip, and once as one figure drawn four times, solid and small at the left, 1-4 and huge at the right. Read that second plate left to right and it is not a specimen sheet: it is a thing coming into being. That is the presence ladder, and it is what a notice's severity rides on.

ToneRungBandGlyphIt says
.cf-notice --presence-faint 1-2 dashed, ink cf-diamond A qualification. Read it or don't.
.cf-notice--achtung --presence-near 2-1 dashed, ink cf-triangle Something will go wrong if you don't.
.cf-notice--fehler --presence-present solid, --feedback-error cf-triangle-solid It already has.

Which is the ladder's own rule obeyed rather than quoted: an object may only move along the scale, never across it. The box, the geometry, the padding and the glyph column are identical in all three. What changes is how solidly the leading edge is drawn — and, between the last two, whether the alert mark is a contour or a body. The warning and the error are the same triangle at two weights, not two different pictures of trouble.

The band is the fourth channel, never the only one. WCAG 1.4.1 is written about colour, but the principle generalises to any single channel a reader may not resolve, and a 1-2 band and a 2-1 band are four pixels apart at arm's length. So every notice states its tone as a word — Hinweis, Achtung, Fehler — which is the same conclusion USWDS reaches from the colour side: “start alert text with the alert type descriptor”. Four channels carry the tone: the word, the glyph, the line type, and on the error the ink. Remove any three and it still reads.

With a way out

A notice that names a dead end should offer the live one. .cf-notice__actions is an optional last row — one or two links, no more. It is a row rather than a slot beside the text because a notice on a phone is 300 px wide and a button parked to the right of a sentence at that width is a button on its own line anyway, one that has pushed the sentence into a 180 px column to get there.

Achtung

Diese Stelle ist seit dem 12. August besetzt.

Alle offenen Stellen Initiativ bewerben

Markup

<div class="cf-notice">
  <!-- the glyph is a part, not a decoration: it is one of the three
       channels the tone is stated on -->
  <svg class="cf-icon cf-icon--sm cf-notice__glyph" aria-hidden="true">
    <use href="#cf-diamond"></use>
  </svg>
  <p class="cf-notice__title">Hinweis</p>
  <div class="cf-notice__body">
    <p>Die Taktzeiten in der Tabelle sind Richtwerte, keine Norm.</p>
  </div>

  <!-- optional -->
  <p class="cf-notice__actions">
    <a href="/karriere">Alle offenen Stellen</a>
  </p>
</div>

The two loud tones are one class on the root: .cf-notice--achtung or .cf-notice--fehler, each with its own glyph from the table above. Hinweis takes no modifier — the quietest tone is what a notice is unless it says otherwise, the same way .cf-btn is the plain button and only the loud ones are named.

The glyph comes from the sprite — either assets/js/cf-icons.js, or the two or three symbols the page actually uses pasted into its own .cf-sprite, which is what blog-artikel.html does. Both work; the script skips a symbol the document already carries.

Which ARIA, and when

The markup above carries none, and that is the common case. A notice that was in the HTML when the page loaded has not happened — nothing changed, so there is nothing to announce, and a live region on it makes the page shout at a reader who is simply reading. What the notice needs depends entirely on how it arrived.

How it arrivedWhat it takesWhy
In the page at load, inside prose nothing It is a paragraph with a frame around it. The title is read in document order like any other line.
In the page at load, at the top of a page or a form role="region" + aria-labelledby on the title Puts it in the landmark list so it can be jumped to. GOV.UK's notification banner does exactly this. One per page at most — a page of landmarks is a page of none.
Server-rendered after a failed POST tabindex="-1" and an id the form's action targets The browser lands on it with nothing running. The same mechanism the error summary uses, and for the same reason: no script, so nothing to fail.
Injected by script, Hinweis or Achtung role="status" Implicitly polite and atomic. Announced at the next pause instead of interrupting whatever is being read.
Injected by script, Fehler role="alert", and no focus move The APG is explicit that alerts do not affect keyboard focus. Something that has to take focus is an alert dialog, and that is a different component.
A live region has to exist before the text does. Injecting a whole element that already carries role="alert" works in most engines and is not guaranteed to; the reliable form is an empty container with the role on it, present in the page, that the message is written into. If a script is going to announce notices, the container is part of the template.

The body sets no font size, and that is the decision. A notice is a sentence inside whatever it stands in — 16 px in .cf-prose, 14 px in a column of supporting copy — not a panel with a type scale of its own. Fixing a size here would make the note in an article visibly smaller than the paragraph above it, which is the one thing a qualification must not look like.

Where it goes

  • Inside an article, next to the thing it qualifies. It ships on patterns/blog-artikel.html, under the latency table, saying that the figures in it are guide values rather than a standard. That is the shape to copy: a note earns its frame by being the sentence a reader would otherwise take the table too literally without.
  • Above a form, when something about the form has changed. Not as standing instructions — those are the field's own hint, where they stay next to the control they describe.
  • On a job page whose position has closed, with the register as its action. The advertisement stays readable; the notice says it is history.
  • Not for field validation. One message per field goes on the field; the list of everything wrong goes in the error summary, which is a different construction with a different job — it links to each field, and a notice does not.
  • Not as a cookie banner. That is Consent, and it is a dialog with legal requirements a notice knows nothing about.
  • Not as an empty state. A page with no results is Error & Empty State — the whole page is the message, and framing it inside a second box says the page worked and something in it did not.
There is no success tone, on purpose. Every system in the field ships one, and this site has nowhere to put it: its successes are whole pages — kontakt-danke and bewerbung-danke — because the thing that just happened is the only thing on the screen. A green box at the top of the form you just submitted is a consolation prize for having failed to navigate anywhere. The rung is available if a page ever genuinely needs it; the tone is not invented until one does.

Why it does not close

There is no dismiss button and no script. Carbon's guidance is the useful half of the argument — reserve the close action for messages with no consequence if they are dismissed, and leave it off anything the reader has to have read. All three of this site's real notices are consequential or permanent: a qualification inside an article is part of the article, a closed vacancy does not reopen when you close the box, and a submission that failed has not succeeded because the message went away. USWDS, which has had the same component for a decade, still ships no dismissible variant either.

What a close button would cost is not the markup. It is a focus decision on every dismissal — the button is removed from the document while it holds focus, and focus then falls to <body> unless something catches it — plus a script on pages that currently need none. That is a real component and worth building the day a page needs one. Today none does.

Accessibility

  • The tone is never carried by one channel. The word, the glyph and the line type all say it, and the error adds ink as a fourth. A reader who resolves none of colour, none of the dash pattern and no icons still gets “FEHLER” as the first word in the box.
  • Contrast. The title and glyph take --notice-ink: full black is 13.5:1 on the page wash, and --feedback-error is 4.71:1 on CF-Grau and 7.33:1 on white — AA at 11 px, which is body text and owes 4.5:1. The band is the same ink, so it clears the 3:1 that non-text contours owe by the margin the type does.
  • The glyph is aria-hidden. It repeats the title word and nothing else; a second announcement of “warning triangle” before the word “Achtung” is noise.
  • Live regions are opt-in, and mostly wrong. The table above is the whole rule. A notice that was in the page at load takes no role, because nothing happened.
  • Nothing here disappears on a timer. WCAG 2.2.3 is the reason, and the APG names it under this exact pattern: an alert that removes itself takes the message away from a reader who was still getting to it.
  • Under forced colours the band survives as a border. A background-image is discarded outright in that mode, so a band painted as a gradient would vanish and all three tones would render as the same box — the failure base.css documents on the rule component. The band becomes a real border-inline-start, whose colour forced colours maps rather than erases, and the two dashed rungs take the two dash styles CSS has: 1-2 renders dotted, 2-1 renders dashed, which is base.css's own mapping. The glyph was not surviving at all, which is the same sentence one channel over: it names --notice-ink on the <svg> itself, and an ink named there is preserved rather than mapped — a <svg> is forced-color-adjust: preserve-parent-color in the UA sheet. Measured with the dark palette: Hinweis and Achtung drew rgb(0, 0, 0) on a Canvas of rgb(0, 0, 0), 1:1; Fehler drew rgb(167, 32, 25) at 2.86:1; the title beside each of them is an ordinary <p> and came through at CanvasText. It inherits now, and the tone still reads, because the three glyphs are three different shapes.
  • No focus states, because there is nothing to focus unless the notice carries actions — and those are ordinary links with the system's ring on them, unchanged.
  • The body is a real block of flow content: paragraphs, a list, a link. Not a single string, so a notice never has to fake a paragraph break with a <br>.

Rules

Do

  • Start the notice with its tone word. It is the channel that survives everything else.
  • Put it next to what it is about — under the table, above the form, on the posting.
  • Keep it to two or three sentences. A notice that needs a heading is a section.
  • Give a dead end a way out with .cf-notice__actions, and cap it at two links.
  • Pick the role from how the notice arrived, not from how important it feels.

Don't

  • Don't fill it, tint it, or put lime in it. Lime is the light layer; a notice is not a light source.
  • Don't invent a fourth tone or a fourth line type. The ladder has four rungs and three of them are spoken for.
  • Don't put role="alert" on a notice that was in the page at load.
  • Don't stack them. Two notices in a row mean neither gets read; make it one, or make it a section.
  • Don't use it for per-field validation, for an empty page, or for consent.
